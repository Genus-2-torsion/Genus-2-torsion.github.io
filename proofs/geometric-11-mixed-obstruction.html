Rational \(11\)-torsion on geometrically split abelian surfaces

19 November 2026

Theorem. Let \(A/\Q\) be an abelian surface isogenous over \(\overline{\Q}\) to a product of elliptic curves. If \(A(\Q)\) contains a point of order \(11\), then \(A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) is cyclic of order \(11\) or \(121\). In particular, for every prime \(\ell\ne11\), a rational point of order \(11\ell\) is impossible on \(A\).

Proof

The structural assertion of the order-\(22\) theorem applies as soon as \(A(\Q)[11]\ne0\). It gives simplicity over \(\Q\) and two geometrically nonisogenous elliptic factors. The two geometric elliptic subvarieties are therefore conjugate over a quadratic field \(K\). Write them as \(E,E^\sigma\), where \(\sigma\) generates \(\operatorname{Gal}(K/\Q)\), and put \(F=A_K/E\). The quotient map restricts to a \(K\)-isogeny \(E^\sigma\to F\). Conjugating gives a \(K\)-isogeny \(E\to F^\sigma\).

For each prime \(r\) dividing \(\#A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\), choose a rational point \(P_r\) of order \(r\). Either \(P_r\) lies in \(E(K)\), or its image in \(F(K)\) has order \(r\). In the second case the conjugate image has order \(r\) in \(F^\sigma(K)\). Thus every such prime occurs as a rational point order in the same elliptic \(K\)-isogeny class, that of \(E\).

Suppose that a second prime \(\ell\ne11\) divides \(\#A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\). Choose curves \(B,C\) in this class carrying \(K\)-points of orders \(11,\ell\), respectively. Factor a \(K\)-isogeny \(f:B\to C\) as \[ B \longrightarrow B'=B/(\ker f)[\ell^\infty] \longrightarrow C . \] The first isogeny preserves the order of the \(11\)-point. The second has degree prime to \(\ell\), so it identifies \(B'[\ell]\) with \(C[\ell]\) over \(K\). Hence \(B'(K)\) has a point of order \(11\ell\). In the quadratic elliptic torsion classification (Kamienny–Najman, Theorem 1, citing Kamienny, Kenku and Momose), the only group of order divisible by \(11\) is \(\mathbf Z/11\mathbf Z\). This contradiction excludes every second prime.

The same classification bounds the orders of \(E(K)[11^\infty]\) and \(F(K)[11^\infty]\) by \(11\). The kernel and image of \(A(\Q)[11^\infty]\to F(K)[11^\infty]\) therefore each have order at most \(11\), so \(\#A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\le121\). Finally, the rational \(p\)-rank theorem for geometrically split surfaces simple over \(\Q\) gives \(\dim_{\mathbf F_{11}}A(\Q)[11]\le1\). This proves cyclicity. ∎

Consequences for the census

Every genus-2 Jacobian over \(\Q\) with a rational point of order \(55\) or \(77\) is geometrically simple. The [55] census row contains the certified geometrically simple curve 55.a. Both split classes are impossible. The exact group \([77]\) has no census row.

The geometrically split Jacobian of curve 11.b realizes exact \([11]\). Existence of a geometrically split Jacobian with exact \([121]\) remains open.
