No rational \(17\)-torsion on geometrically split abelian surfaces

10 November 2026 · Geometrically split

Theorem. Let \(A/\Q\) be a geometrically split abelian surface. Then \(A(\Q)\) contains no point of order \(17\).

Corollary. No geometrically split genus-\(2\) Jacobian over \(\Q\) has a rational point of order \(17\). Equivalently, a genus-\(2\) Jacobian over \(\Q\) with a rational point of order \(17\) is geometrically simple. In particular, [17] does not occur as the rational torsion subgroup of a geometrically split genus-\(2\) Jacobian over \(\Q\).

Here “geometrically split” means that \(A_{\bar\Q}\) is isogenous to a product of two elliptic curves. We impose no restriction on the degree of this isogeny, on the field of definition of the geometric endomorphisms, or on the integral endomorphism ring; we do not assume that \(A\) is principally polarized, and we do not assume that \(A\) is simple over \(\Q\). For surfaces that are isogenous over \(\Q\) to a product of two elliptic curves the theorem follows from Mazur's theorem (prime torsion obstruction); the new case is that of surfaces that are simple over \(\Q\). The theorem contains the statement about order \(34\) of the proof for orders 33 and 34.

The proof distinguishes cases according to the geometric endomorphism algebra (Section 2). Most cases are settled by the known results on torsion of elliptic curves over fields of degree at most \(2\) (Section 3), by counting points on a reduction at \(2\) (Section 4), or by comparing lattices in the \(17\)-adic Tate module (Section 5). In the one remaining case the geometric endomorphisms are defined over a cyclic quartic field. There a rational point of order \(17\) produces a non-cuspidal point of the modular curve \(X_0(34)\) over a real quadratic field \(K\) which the Atkin–Lehner involution \(w_2\) maps to its conjugate (Section 6). The rational points of \(X_0(34)/w_2\) show that such a point exists only for \(K=\Q(\sqrt{-15})\) (Section 7).

The Magma script and its log cited below are published with this proof, as geometric_17_checks.m and geometric_17_checks.log.

1. Conventions and standard facts

All number fields are subfields of a fixed algebraic closure \(\bar\Q\) of \(\Q\). For a field \(k\) we write \(G_k=\Gal(\bar k/k)\). For a prime \(\ell\), \(\chi_\ell\) is the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character and \(\bar\chi_\ell\) its reduction modulo \(\ell\); thus \(\bar\chi_{17}:G_{\Q}\to\F_{17}^\times\) is surjective of order \(16\). For an abelian variety \(X\) over a field \(k\) of characteristic \(0\), \(T_\ell X\) is its \(\ell\)-adic Tate module, \(V_\ell X=T_\ell X\otimes\Q_\ell\), \(\End^0=\End\otimes\Q\), \(\Hom^0=\Hom\otimes\Q\), and \(X\sim_k Y\) means that \(X\) and \(Y\) are isogenous over \(k\). A quasi-isogeny is an invertible element of \(\Hom^0\). An elliptic curve is “without CM” if its endomorphism ring over \(\bar\Q\) is \(\Z\). For a \(G\)-stable \(\Z_\ell\)-lattice \(\Lambda\) in a \(\Q_\ell[G]\)-module we put \(\bar\Lambda=\Lambda/\ell\Lambda\), and \((\cdot)^{\mathrm{ss}}\) denotes semisimplification. A “constituent” is a Jordan–Hölder factor.

Conjugates. For a variety \(Y\) over \(\bar\Q\) and \(g\in G_{\Q}\), \(Y^g\) is the base change of \(Y\) along \(g:\bar\Q\to\bar\Q\), and \(g:Y(\bar\Q)\to Y^g(\bar\Q)\) is the induced bijection. A morphism \(f:Y\to Z\) has the conjugate \(g(f):Y^g\to Z^g\), characterized by \(g(f)(g(P))=g(f(P))\). We have \((Y^g)^h=Y^{hg}\). If \(Y\) is defined over a number field \(k\), then \(Y^g\) depends only on the restriction of \(g\) to \(k\), and \(Y^g=Y\) for \(g\in G_k\); a morphism \(f\) between varieties defined over \(k\) is defined over \(k\) if and only if \(g(f)=f\) for all \(g\in G_k\). Conjugation is compatible with composition, with duals of isogenies and with degrees.

For a character \(\eta\) of \(G_K\), where \(K\) is a number field, and \(\tau\in G_{\Q}\) normalizing \(G_K\), we put \(\eta^\tau(g)=\eta(\tau^{-1}g\tau)\); similarly, for a \(G_K\)-module \(M\), \(M^\tau\) is \(M\) with \(g\) acting as \(\tau^{-1}g\tau\). If \(X\) is an abelian variety over \(K\), then \(\tau\) induces an isomorphism of \(G_K\)-modules \(X[n]^\tau\cong X^\tau[n]\), because \(\tau(g'v)=(\tau g'\tau^{-1})\tau(v)\) for \(g'\in G_K\).

We use the following standard facts.

  • (F1) Degrees. The degree extends multiplicatively to quasi-isogenies between abelian varieties of dimension \(d\), by \(\deg(f/n)=\deg(f)/n^{2d}\). Let \(X\) be an elliptic curve over \(\bar\Q\) without CM, so that \(\End(X^2)=M_2(\Z)\). An element \(N\in M_2(\Z)\) with \(\det N\neq0\) has degree \((\det N)^2\): write \(N=U\,\mathrm{diag}(d_1,d_2)\,U'\) with \(U,U'\in\GL_2(\Z)\); the outer factors are automorphisms and the middle one has degree \(d_1^2d_2^2\). Hence \(\deg N=(\det N)^2\) for all \(N\in\GL_2(\Q)\). If \(\mu:X'\to X\) is an isogeny of elliptic curves, then \(\mathrm{diag}(\mu,\mu):X'^2\to X^2\) has degree \((\deg\mu)^2\).

  • (F2) Brauer–Nesbitt. For a continuous representation of a profinite group \(G\) on a finite-dimensional \(\Q_\ell\)-vector space \(V\), the semisimplification \(\bar\Lambda^{\mathrm{ss}}\) does not depend on the \(G\)-stable lattice \(\Lambda\subset V\). The same applies to two isomorphic representations, after transporting a lattice through the isomorphism. Indeed, after scaling we may assume \(\Lambda'\subseteq\Lambda\); the \(G\)-stable lattices \(M_i=\Lambda'+\ell^i\Lambda\) satisfy \(M_0=\Lambda\), \(M_i=\Lambda'\) for large \(i\), and \(\ell M_i\subseteq M_{i+1}\subseteq M_i\). For \(G\)-stable lattices \(M'\subseteq M\) with \(\ell M\subseteq M'\), both \(M/\ell M\) and \(M'/\ell M'\) have the composition factors of \(M/M'\) and of \(M'/\ell M\): the first contains \(M'/\ell M\) with quotient \(M/M'\), the second contains \(\ell M/\ell M'\cong M/M'\) with quotient \(M'/\ell M\).

  • (F3) Forms of a square. Let \(k\) be a number field, \(E\) an elliptic curve over \(k\) without CM, \(B\) an abelian surface over \(k\), and \(f:E^2_{\bar k}\to B_{\bar k}\) a quasi-isogeny. For \(g\in G_k\) put

    \[ \rho(g)=f^{-1}\circ g(f)\in\operatorname{Aut}^0(E^2_{\bar k})=\GL_2(\Q). \]

    1. \(\rho:G_k\to\GL_2(\Q)\) is a homomorphism with finite image. Indeed \(\rho(gh)\)\({}=f^{-1}g(f)\cdot g\bigl(f^{-1}h(f)\bigr)\)\({}=\rho(g)\rho(h)\), because the elements of \(M_2(\Q)=\End^0(E^2_{\bar k})\) are defined over \(k\); and \(\rho\) is trivial on \(G_{k'}\) for a finite extension \(k'\) of \(k\) over which \(f\) is defined.

    2. For \(\varphi\in\End^0(B_{\bar k})\) we have \(f^{-1}g(\varphi)f\)\({}=\rho(g)\,(f^{-1}\varphi f)\,\rho(g)^{-1}\), as one sees by applying \(g\) to \(f^{-1}\varphi f\in M_2(\Q)\). So, in the identification \(\End^0(B_{\bar k})\cong M_2(\Q)\) given by \(f\), the element \(g\) acts by \(\mathrm{Ad}(\rho(g))\), and \(\End^0_k(B)\) corresponds to the commutant of \(\rho(G_k)\) in \(M_2(\Q)\).

    3. \(f\) induces an isomorphism \(V_\ell(E)\otimes_{\Q}\Q^2=V_\ell(E^2)\)\({}\to V_\ell(B)\) under which \(g\in G_k\) acts on the left-hand side by \(\rho_{E,\ell}(g)\otimes\rho(g)\): indeed \(g(f(v))=g(f)(gv)\)\({}=f\bigl(\rho(g)(gv)\bigr)\).

  • (F4) Elliptic curves over fields of degree at most \(2\). No elliptic curve over \(\Q\) or over a quadratic field has a rational point of order \(17\). Over \(\Q\) this is part of Mazur's theorem [Maz, Theorem (8)]. Over quadratic fields it is Kamienny's theorem [Kam, Theorem 3.1]: the order of the torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve over a quadratic field is not divisible by any prime greater than \(13\). (In the notation of [DKSS, Section 1], where \(S(d)\) is the set of primes that occur as the order of a point on an elliptic curve over a number field of degree \(d\): \(S(1)=\mathrm{Primes}(7)\) and \(S(2)=\mathrm{Primes}(13)\).)

Lemma 1. Let \(X\) be an abelian surface over a number field \(k\), let \(E\subset X\) be an elliptic curve defined over \(k\), and let \(Q\in X(k)\) have prime order \(q\). Then either \(Q\in E(k)\), or the image of \(Q\) in \((X/E)(k)\) has order \(q\).

Proof. The image of \(Q\) is zero exactly when \(Q\in E\). ∎

2. The case distinction

Let \(A/\Q\) be a geometrically split abelian surface, with \(A_{\bar\Q}\sim E_1\times E_2\). Exactly one of the following holds:

  • (N) \(E_1\) and \(E_2\) are not isogenous;

  • (CM) \(E_1\sim E_2=:E\) and \(E\) has complex multiplication;

  • (S) \(E_1\sim E_2=:E\) and \(E\) has no complex multiplication.

In case (S), \(D:=\End^0(A_{\bar\Q})\cong M_2(\Q)\). Let \(L\) be the smallest field over which all geometric endomorphisms of \(A\) are defined; it is a finite Galois extension of \(\Q\). The group \(\Gamma=\Gal(L/\Q)\) acts faithfully on \(D\), and \(\End^0_{\Q}(A)=D^\Gamma\). By Skolem–Noether this action embeds \(\Gamma\) into \(\operatorname{Aut}(M_2(\Q))=\PGL_2(\Q)\); we write \(g\mapsto[R_g]\), so that \(g\) acts on \(D\) by \(\mathrm{Ad}(R_g)\); for \(g\in G_{\Q}\) we put \(R_g=R_{g|_L}\). The trace and the determinant of an element of \(D\) are those of the corresponding matrix; they do not depend on the isomorphism \(D\cong M_2(\Q)\).

A finite subgroup of \(\PGL_2(\mathbb R)\) is cyclic or dihedral: \(\PGL_2(\mathbb R)\) acts on the hyperbolic plane by isometries (an element of negative determinant by \(z\mapsto(a\bar z+b)/(c\bar z+d)\)), a finite subgroup fixes a point, and the stabilizer of a point is isomorphic to \(O(2)\). If \([R]\in\PGL_2(\Q)\) has order \(n>2\), the ratio of the eigenvalues of \(R\) is a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), and \((\tr R)^2/\det R\)\({}=2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\in\Q\); hence \(n\in\{3,4,6\}\). Therefore

\[ \Gamma\in\{C_1,\ C_2,\ C_3,\ C_4,\ C_6,\ V_4,\ D_6,\ D_8,\ D_{12}\}, \]

where \(D_{2n}\) is dihedral of order \(2n\) (so \(D_6\cong S_3\)) and \(V_4\) is the Klein four group. An element \(r\) of order \(n\in\{3,4,6\}\) has a unique representative \(R\) with \(\tr R=1\) (the trace is nonzero, since otherwise \(R^2\) would be a scalar); its characteristic polynomial is \(t^2-t+1/c\) with \(c=1,2,3\) respectively, of discriminant \(-3,-1,-1/3\). Since \(R(1-R)=1/c\), the trace-one representative of \(r^{-1}\) is \(1-R\).

Every elliptic subvariety of \(A_{\bar\Q}\) is the image of an integral multiple of a rank-one idempotent of \(D\), so it is defined over \(L\). If \(E,E'\subset A_L\) are elliptic subvarieties, every geometric homomorphism \(E'\to E\) is defined over \(L\): composing with an \(L\)-rational quasi-projection \(A\to E'\) and the inclusion \(E\subset A\) embeds \(\Hom^0(E'_{\bar\Q},E_{\bar\Q})\) \(G_L\)-equivariantly into \(D\), on which \(G_L\) acts trivially.

The Theorem is proved case by case: type (N), and type (S) with \(\Gamma\in\{C_1,C_2\}\), in Section 3; type (CM) in Section 4; type (S) with \(\Gamma\in\{C_3,C_6,V_4,D_6,D_8,D_{12}\}\) in Section 5; and type (S) with \(\Gamma=C_4\) in Sections 6 and 7. The proof is assembled in Section 8.

3. Elliptic curves over fields of degree at most \(2\)

Lemma 2. Let \(A\) be an abelian surface over \(\Q\) and \(k\) a number field with \([k:\Q]\le2\) such that \(A_k\) contains an elliptic curve defined over \(k\). Then \(A(\Q)\) contains no point of order \(17\).

Proof. Let \(E\subset A_k\) be an elliptic curve defined over \(k\), and suppose that \(Q\in A(\Q)\) has order \(17\). By Lemma 1 over \(k\), either \(Q\in E(k)\) or the image of \(Q\) in \((A_k/E)(k)\) has order \(17\). Both \(E\) and \(A_k/E\) are elliptic curves over a field of degree at most \(2\), which contradicts (F4). ∎

Type (N). Let \(A\) be of type (N). Then \(A_{\bar\Q}\) has exactly two elliptic subvarieties: an elliptic subvariety is isogenous to \(E_1\) or to \(E_2\), and for each \(i\) the space \(\Hom^0(E_i,A_{\bar\Q})\) has rank one over the field \(\End^0(E_i)\), so all nonzero maps \(E_i\to A_{\bar\Q}\) have the same image \(B_i\); and \(B_1\neq B_2\) because they are not isogenous. The group \(G_{\Q}\) permutes \(B_1\) and \(B_2\). The stabilizer of \(B_1\) is an open subgroup of index at most \(2\); its fixed field \(k\) has degree at most \(2\), and \(B_1\) is defined over \(k\). By Lemma 2, \(A(\Q)\) has no point of order \(17\).

Type (S) with \(\Gamma\in\{C_1,C_2\}\). Here \([L:\Q]\le2\), and \(A_{\bar\Q}\sim E^2\) contains an elliptic subvariety, which is defined over \(L\) by Section 2. By Lemma 2 with \(k=L\), \(A(\Q)\) has no point of order \(17\).

This argument does not extend to all the remaining groups \(\Gamma\): for \([L:\Q]=4\) the analogue of (F4) fails, since elliptic curves over quartic fields can have a point of order \(17\) (see Remark 1 in Section 9).

4. Potentially good reduction at \(2\)

Proposition 3. Let \(A\) be an abelian surface over \(\Q\) with potentially good reduction at \(2\). Then \(A(\Q)\) contains no point of order \(17\).

Here “potentially good reduction at \(2\)” means that \(A\) acquires good reduction over a finite extension \(F'\) of \(\Q_2\); for example \(F'=k_v\) when \(A_k\) has good reduction at a prime \(v\mid2\) of a number field \(k\). We fix an embedding \(\bar\Q\subset\bar\Q_2\), so that \(G_{\Q_2}\subseteq G_{\Q}\).

Proof.

  1. Good reduction over a totally ramified extension of \(\Q_2\). Let \(I\subset G_{\Q_2}\) be the inertia group. By the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion [ST, Theorem 1], the inertia group \(I\cap G_{F'}\) of \(F'\) acts trivially on \(T_3A\); as it has finite index in \(I\), the group \(I\) acts on \(T_3A\) through a finite quotient. Put \(N=I\cap\ker(\rho_{A,3})\), an open normal subgroup of \(I\) that is normal in \(G_{\Q_2}\). The group \(G_{\Q_2}/N\) is an extension of \(G_{\Q_2}/I\cong\hat\Z\) by the finite group \(I/N\). Since \(\hat\Z\) is a free profinite group on one generator, the extension splits: mapping the generator to a lift of the Frobenius element defines a continuous section. So there is a closed subgroup \(H\supseteq N\) of \(G_{\Q_2}\) with \(H\cap I=N\) and \(HI=G_{\Q_2}\); it has index \([I:N]\). Its fixed field \(F\) is a finite extension of \(\Q_2\) with residue field \(\F_2\), i.e. totally ramified, and its inertia group \(N\) acts trivially on \(T_3A\). By the same criterion, \(A\) has good reduction \(\bar A/\F_2\) over \(F\). Prime-to-\(2\) torsion of \(A(F)\) injects into \(\bar A(\F_2)\): for odd \(m\), the \(m\)-torsion of the Néron model of \(A_F\), an abelian scheme over the ring of integers \(\mathcal O_F\), is finite étale over \(\mathcal O_F\), so reduction is injective on \(A(F)[m]\). So, if \(A(\Q)\) has a point of order \(17\), then \(17\) divides \(\#\bar A(\F_2)=P(1)\), where \(P\) is the characteristic polynomial of the Frobenius endomorphism \(\pi\) of \(\bar A\).

  2. The Weil polynomial. The polynomial \(P\in\Z[T]\) is monic of degree \(4\), its complex roots have absolute value \(\sqrt2\) by Weil's theorem, and \(P(0)=\deg\pi=4\). The non-real roots form pairs \(\omega,\bar\omega\) with \(\omega\bar\omega=2\); each pair contributes a factor \(T^2-\beta T+2\) with \(\beta=\omega+\bar\omega\) real and \(|\beta|<2\sqrt2\). The possible real roots \(\sqrt2\) and \(-\sqrt2\) are conjugate over \(\Q\), so they have the same multiplicity \(m\in\{0,1,2\}\). If \(m=1\), then \(P=(T^2-2)(T^2-\beta T+2)\) and \(P(0)=-4\), which is excluded. If \(m=2\), then \(P=(T^2-2)^2\)\({}=(T^2-2\sqrt2\,T+2)(T^2\)\({}+2\sqrt2\,T+2)\). In all cases

    \[ P(T)=(T^2-\beta_1T+2)(T^2-\beta_2T+2)=T^4+aT^3+bT^2+2aT+4 \]

    with real numbers \(|\beta_i|\le2\sqrt2\) and integers \(a=-(\beta_1+\beta_2)\), \(b=\beta_1\beta_2+4\), and

    \[ P(1)=5+3a+b=(3-\beta_1)(3-\beta_2). \]

  3. \(P(1)\) is not divisible by \(17\). Put \(x_i=3-\beta_i\). Then \(0<3-2\sqrt2\le x_i\)\({}\le3+2\sqrt2\), so \(P(1)=x_1x_2\) is a positive integer and \(P(1)\le(3+2\sqrt2)^2\)\({}=17+12\sqrt2<34\), because \(288<289\). Suppose that \(17\) divides \(P(1)\). Then \(x_1x_2=17\), and both \(x_i\) lie in the interval \([\,17/(3+2\sqrt2),\ 3+2\sqrt2\,]\)\({}=[\,51-34\sqrt2,\ 3+2\sqrt2\,]\). The function \(x\mapsto x+17/x\) is convex on this interval and takes the same value \(54-32\sqrt2\) at both endpoints, which are exchanged by \(x\mapsto17/x\); and it is at least \(2\sqrt{17}\). Hence the integer \(x_1+x_2=6+a\) satisfies

    \[ 8<2\sqrt{17}\ \le\ 6+a\ \le\ 54-32\sqrt2<9, \]

    where the outer inequalities are \(64<68\) and \(2025<2048\). This is impossible. ∎

As a check, part (A) of the script geometric_17_checks.m (see Section 7) enumerates all pairs of integers \((a,b)\) for which \(T^4+aT^3+bT^2+2aT+4\) has all its roots of absolute value \(\sqrt2\). There are \(35\) of them, and the values of \(P(1)\) are \(1,\dots,16,19,20,25\).

Type (CM). Let \(A\) be of type (CM). The curve \(E\) has potentially good reduction everywhere, because it has CM (its \(j\)-invariant is an algebraic integer; see [ST, §2]). Over a suitable number field \(k\), \(A_k\) is isogenous to \(E_k^2\) and \(E_k\) has good reduction at the primes above \(2\). For a prime \(\ell\neq2\), \(V_\ell(A_k)\cong V_\ell(E_k)^2\) as \(G_k\)-modules, and this is unramified at every prime \(v\mid2\) of \(k\); so \(A_k\) has good reduction at \(v\) by the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion [ST, Theorem 1]. So \(A\) has potentially good reduction at \(2\), and by Proposition 3, \(A(\Q)\) has no point of order \(17\).

5. Type (S): lattices in the Tate module

In this section \(A\) is of type (S), \(p\) is an odd prime, \(V=V_p(A)\) and \(T=T_p(A)\). The group \(G_{\Q}\) acts on \(D\otimes\Q_p\) through \(\Gamma\), and \(g(xv)=g(x)\,g(v)\) for \(x\in D\otimes\Q_p\) and \(v\in V\). We fix an isomorphism \(D\otimes\Q_p\cong\End_{\Q_p}(S)\) with \(S=\Q_p^2\); as a \(D\otimes\Q_p\)-module, \(V\) is isomorphic to \(S\oplus S\).

Lemma 4 (polarization). Let \(T'\subset V\) be a \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice such that \((T'/pT')|_{G_L}\) has trivial semisimplification. Then \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subseteq L\).

Proof. A polarization of \(A\) defined over \(\Q\) gives an isomorphism of \(G_{\Q}\)-modules \(V\cong\Hom(V,\Q_p(1))\). Transport the lattice \(\Hom(T',\Z_p(1))\) into \(V\). Its reduction is \(\Hom(T'/pT',\mu_p)\), whose semisimplification as a \(G_L\)-module consists of four copies of \(\bar\chi_p|_{G_L}\), while that of \(T'/pT'\) consists of four copies of the trivial character. By (F2) over \(G_L\), \(\bar\chi_p|_{G_L}=1\), that is, \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subseteq L\), since \(\Q(\zeta_p)\) is the fixed field of \(\ker\bar\chi_p\). The degree of the polarization plays no role. ∎

Lemma 5 (unramified order). Suppose that \(\Gamma\) normalizes an unramified quadratic \(\Z_p\)-order \(\mathcal B\subset D\otimes\Q_p\) (the ring of integers of an unramified quadratic extension of \(\Q_p\) contained in \(D\otimes\Q_p\)), that some \(g_0\in\Gamma\) acts on \(D\otimes\Q_p\) by \(\mathrm{Ad}(R_0)\) for a unit \(R_0\in\mathcal B^\times\) whose reduction lies in \(\F_{p^2}\setminus\F_p\), and that \(A(\Q)[p]\neq0\). Then \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subseteq L\).

Proof.

  1. A \(\Gamma\)-stable maximal order. The field \(\mathcal B\otimes\Q_p\) acts on \(S\), which is a one-dimensional vector space over it. Since \(\mathcal B\) is a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer \(p\), the \(\mathcal B\)-stable lattices in \(S\) are the lattices \(p^k\Lambda\), \(k\in\Z\), where \(\Lambda=\mathcal Bv_0\) for a fixed nonzero \(v_0\in S\). For \(g\in\Gamma\) choose any \(R_g\in(D\otimes\Q_p)^\times\) inducing the action of \(g\). Then \(R_g\Lambda\) is stable under \(R_g\mathcal BR_g^{-1}=\mathcal B\), so \(R_g\Lambda=p^{k(g)}\Lambda\), and \(M_g:=p^{-k(g)}R_g\) lies in \(\GL(\Lambda)\). It induces the action of \(g\) on \(D\otimes\Q_p\) and is determined by \(g\) up to a factor in \(\Z_p^\times\). Consequently \(\Gamma\) preserves the maximal order \(\mathcal R=\End(\Lambda)\cong M_2(\Z_p)\), which contains \(\mathcal B\), and \(g\mapsto[\bar M_g]\in\PGL(U)\) is a homomorphism, where \(U=\Lambda/p\Lambda\) and \(\bar M_g\) is the reduction of \(M_g\).

  2. Tensor decomposition. Put \(T'=\mathcal RT\). It is a \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice in \(V\) and an \(\mathcal R\)-module. Choose a basis \(\lambda_1,\lambda_2\) of \(\Lambda\) and let \(e_{ik}\in\mathcal R\) be the matrix units, \(e_{ik}\lambda_l=\delta_{kl}\lambda_i\). Then \(T'=e_{11}T'\oplus e_{22}T'\), and \(e_{21}:e_{11}T'\to e_{22}T'\) is an isomorphism with inverse \(e_{12}\). So \(W_0=e_{11}T'\) is free of rank \(2\) over \(\Z_p\), and

    \[ \Lambda\otimes_{\Z_p}W_0\to T',\qquad\lambda_l\otimes w\mapsto e_{l1}w, \]

    is an isomorphism of \(\mathcal R\)-modules. Let \(\bar V=T'/pT'\) and \(W=W_0/pW_0\). Then \(\bar V\cong U\otimes W\) as modules over \(\mathcal R/p\mathcal R=\End(U)\). For \(g\in G_{\Q}\), let \(\bar g\) be its action on \(\bar V\), and write \(M_g\) for the matrix attached to the image of \(g\) in \(\Gamma\). From \(g(xv)=M_gxM_g^{-1}\,g(v)\) for \(x\in\mathcal R\) and \(v\in T'\) we get \(\bar g\circ(\bar x\otimes1)\)\({}=(\bar M_g\bar x\bar M_g^{-1}\otimes1)\circ\bar g\) for all \(\bar x\in\End(U)\). Hence \((\bar M_g^{-1}\otimes1)\circ\bar g\) commutes with \(\End(U)\otimes1\), so it is of the form \(1\otimes N_g\) with \(N_g\in\GL(W)\), and \(\bar g=\bar M_g\otimes N_g\). Let

    \[ \tilde G=\{(g,M)\in G_{\Q}\times\GL(U)\ :\ M\in\F_p^\times\bar M_g\}. \]

    This is a group, by step 1. For \((g,M)\in\tilde G\) there is a unique \(N\in\GL(W)\) with \(\bar g=M\otimes N\), and \((g,M)\mapsto N\) is a homomorphism, since \(\bar g\bar g'=MM'\otimes NN'\). So \(U\) and \(W\) are two-dimensional \(\tilde G\)-modules, and \(\bar V\cong U\otimes W\) as \(\tilde G\)-modules, where \(\tilde G\) acts on \(\bar V\) through its projection to \(G_{\Q}\).

  3. \(U\) is irreducible. We may take \(M_{g_0}=R_0\), since \(R_0\Lambda=\Lambda\). So \(\tilde G\) contains an element acting on \(U\cong\mathcal B/p\mathcal B=\F_{p^2}\) as multiplication by an element of \(\F_{p^2}\setminus\F_p\), which has no eigenvector in \(U\).

  4. \(W\) is irreducible. The lattices \(T\) and \(T'\) have the same reduction up to semisimplification, by (F2). As \(A(\Q)[p]\neq0\), the trivial character is a constituent of \(T/pT\), hence of \(\bar V\). If \(W\) had a \(\tilde G\)-stable line \(W_1\), then \(U\otimes W_1\) and \(U\otimes W/W_1\) would be irreducible \(\tilde G\)-modules of dimension \(2\), and \(\bar V\) would have no one-dimensional \(\tilde G\)-constituent, hence no one-dimensional \(G_{\Q}\)-constituent: \(\tilde G\) maps onto \(G_{\Q}\), since it contains \((g,\bar M_g)\) for every \(g\), and it acts on \(\bar V\) through this map.

  5. \(G_L\) acts trivially on \(\bar V\). For \(h\in G_L\), \(\bar M_h\) is a scalar, so \(H=\{(h,1):h\in G_L\}\) is a subgroup of \(\tilde G\). It is normal, because \(G_L\) is normal in \(G_{\Q}\), and it acts trivially on \(U\). By Clifford's theorem, \(W|_H\) is semisimple: the \(\tilde G\)-translates of a simple \(H\)-submodule are simple \(H\)-submodules whose sum is a nonzero \(\tilde G\)-submodule, hence all of \(W\). Since \(\bar V|_H\cong W|_H\oplus W|_H\) has a trivial constituent (the restriction of a trivial constituent of \(\bar V\)) and \(W|_H\) is semisimple, \(W^H\neq0\); as \(W^H\) is \(\tilde G\)-stable and \(W\) is irreducible, \(W^H=W\). So \(G_L\) acts trivially on \(\bar V=T'/pT'\).

Lemma 6 (split blocks). Suppose that \(\Gamma\) normalizes a split étale order \(\mathcal B=\Z_pe_1\oplus\Z_pe_2\subset D\otimes\Q_p\), where \(e_1,e_2\) are nonzero orthogonal idempotents with \(e_1+e_2=1\), and that some element of \(\Gamma\) exchanges \(e_1\) and \(e_2\). Let \(K\subset L\) be the fixed field of the subgroup of index \(2\) of \(\Gamma\) that fixes \(e_1\). Then \(\Gal(L/K)\) acts on the line \(e_1(D\otimes\Q_p)e_2\) by a character \(\eta\). If \(\eta\neq1\) and \(A(\Q)[p]\neq0\), then \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subseteq L\).

Proof. Every element of \(\Gamma\) permutes the two primitive idempotents \(e_1,e_2\) of \(\mathcal B\); the elements fixing \(e_1\) also fix \(e_2=1-e_1\) and hence preserve the line \(e_1(D\otimes\Q_p)e_2\). The character \(\eta\) has finite order, so its values are roots of unity in \(\Z_p^\times\), on which reduction modulo \(p\) is injective; thus \(\bar\eta\neq1\). We regard \(\eta\) as a character of \(G_K\).

Put \(T'=\mathcal BT=T_1\oplus T_2\) with \(T_i=e_iT\); it is a \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice. Let \(M_i=T_i/pT_i\) and \(V_i=T_i\otimes\Q_p=e_iV\), of dimension \(2\). The group \(G_K\) preserves each \(T_i\), and an element \(\tau\in G_{\Q}\setminus G_K\) exchanges them, so that \(M_2\cong M_1^{\tau}\). A nonzero \(\xi\in e_1(D\otimes\Q_p)e_2\) maps \(V_2\) isomorphically onto \(V_1\) and satisfies \(g(\xi v)=\eta(g)\,\xi\,g(v)\) for \(g\in G_K\). So \(\xi(T_2)\) is a \(G_K\)-stable lattice in \(V_1\) with reduction \(\bar\eta\otimes M_2\), and by (F2), \(M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}\cong\bar\eta\otimes M_2^{\mathrm{ss}}\).

The rational point of order \(p\) gives a trivial constituent of \(T/pT\), hence, by (F2), a trivial \(G_K\)-constituent of \(M_1\oplus M_2\). Since \(M_2\cong M_1^\tau\), both \(M_i\) have a trivial constituent. Writing \(M_2^{\mathrm{ss}}=\mathbf 1\oplus\alpha\), we get \(M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}=\bar\eta\oplus\bar\eta\alpha\ni\mathbf 1\), so \(\alpha=\bar\eta^{-1}\). All four constituents \(\mathbf 1,\bar\eta^{-1},\bar\eta,\mathbf 1\) of \(T'/pT'\) are trivial on \(G_L\), and Lemma 4 applies. ∎

7. The curve \(X_0(34)\)

Standard facts. Let \(j\) be the modular \(j\)-function on the upper half-plane \(\mathfrak H\), with \(q\)-expansion \(q^{-1}+744+196884q+\cdots\) in \(q=e^{2\pi iz}\), and put \(j_d(z)=j(dz)\) for an integer \(d\ge1\). We use the following standard facts about the modular curve \(X_0(N)\); see for example [DS, Section 1.5 and Sections 7.5–7.7] and [DI, Part II].

  • (M1) The field of modular functions for \(\Gamma_0(N)\) is \(\mathbb C(j,j_N)\). Its degree over \(\mathbb C(j)\) is the index of \(\Gamma_0(N)\) in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\), which is \(54\) for \(N=34\). The curve \(X_0(N)\) is the smooth projective curve over \(\Q\) with function field \(\Q(j,j_N)\); its complex points are \(\Gamma_0(N)\backslash\mathfrak H^*\), and its cusps are the poles of \(j\). For a prime \(N\) there are two cusps, \(\infty\) and \(0\); they are exchanged by the Fricke involution \(z\mapsto-1/(Nz)\), which exchanges \(j\) and \(j_N\).

  • (M2) The non-cuspidal points of \(X_0(N)(\bar\Q)\) correspond to the \(\bar\Q\)-isomorphism classes of pairs \((E,C)\), where \(E\) is an elliptic curve over \(\bar\Q\) and \(C\subset E\) is a cyclic subgroup of order \(N\). The correspondence is compatible with the action of \(G_{\Q}\), where \((E,C)^g=(E^g,g(C))\); in particular a pair defined over a number field \(K\) gives a point of \(X_0(N)(K)\). (For pairs without CM, the only ones used below, this is proved after the list.) At the point \((E,C)\) the function \(j\) takes the value \(j(E)\) and, for \(d\mid N\), the function \(j_d\) takes the value \(j(E/C[d])\). Over \(\mathbb C\), the orbit of \(z\in\mathfrak H\) corresponds to the pair \((\mathbb C/(\Z+\Z z),\langle1/N\rangle)\).

  • (M3) The matrix \(W_2=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\34&18\end{pmatrix}\), of determinant \(2\), normalizes \(\Gamma_0(34)\), and \(z\mapsto W_2z\) induces the Atkin–Lehner involution \(w_2\) of \(X_0(34)\). It is an automorphism of order \(2\) defined over \(\Q\), and on non-cuspidal points it is given by

    \[ w_2(E,C)=\bigl(E/C[2],\ (C+E[2])/C[2]\bigr); \]

    in particular \(j\circ w_2=j_2\).

The compatibility in (M2) for pairs without CM. (a) If \(E\) has no CM, then \((E,C)\) is determined up to isomorphism by \(j(E)\) and \(j(E/C)\). Indeed, let \(C,C'\subset E\) be cyclic of order \(N\) with \(E/C\cong E/C'\), let \(\pi,\pi'\) be the quotient maps and \(\theta:E/C\to E/C'\) an isomorphism. Then \(\hat\pi'\circ\theta\circ\pi\in\End(E)=\Z\) has degree \(N^2\), so it equals \(\pm N=\pm\hat\pi'\circ\pi'\). Hence \(\hat\pi'\circ(\theta\circ\pi\mp\pi')=0\); as \(\hat\pi'\) has finite kernel and \(E\) is connected, \(\theta\circ\pi=\pm\pi'\), and \(C=\ker\pi=\ker\pi'=C'\). (b) Since \(j\) and \(j_N\) lie in \(\Q(X_0(N))\), we have \(h(g(P))=g(h(P))\) for \(h\in\{j,j_N\}\), \(g\in G_{\Q}\) and \(P\in X_0(N)(\bar\Q)\) not a cusp. If \(P\) corresponds to \((E,C)\) with \(E\) without CM, then \(g(P)\) and the point corresponding to \((E^g,g(C))\) both have \(j\)-value \(g(j(E))=j(E^g)\) and \(j_N\)-value \(g(j(E/C))=j(E^g/g(C))\); since \(E^g\) has no CM, they coincide by (a). In particular a pair without CM defined over \(K\) gives a point fixed by \(G_K\), that is, a point of \(X_0(N)(K)\).

The two descriptions of \(w_2\) in (M3) agree, as the following computation shows. (That \(w_2\) is defined over \(\Q\) is not used below; it also follows from Lemma 8.) Put \(z'=W_2z\)\({}=(2z+1)/(34z+18)\). Since \((34z+18)-17(2z+1)=1\), we have \(\Z(34z+18)+\Z(2z+1)\)\({}=\Z+\Z\cdot2z\), so multiplication by \(34z+18\) is an isomorphism \(\mathbb C/(\Z+\Z z')\)\({}\to\mathbb C/(\Z+\Z\cdot2z)\). It maps \(1/34\) to \(z+9/17\), which generates the subgroup \(\langle z\rangle+\langle1/17\rangle\), because \(17(z+9/17)\equiv z\) and \(2(z+9/17)\equiv1/17\) modulo \(\Z+\Z\cdot2z\). On the other hand, for \(E=\mathbb C/(\Z+\Z z)\) and \(C=\langle1/34\rangle\), multiplication by \(2\) is an isomorphism \(E/C[2]=\mathbb C/(\tfrac12\Z+\Z z)\)\({}\to\mathbb C/(\Z+\Z\cdot2z)\); it maps \((C+E[2])/C[2]\), which is generated by \(1/34\) and \(z/2\), onto \(\langle1/17\rangle+\langle z\rangle\).

Lemma 8 (the model). There is an isomorphism \(\Theta:\mathcal X\to X_0(34)\) of curves over \(\Q\) such that \(j\circ\Theta=j_{\mathcal X}\) and \(\Theta\circ\omega_2=w_2\circ\Theta\). In particular \(\Theta\) maps the poles of \(j_{\mathcal X}\) onto the cusps of \(X_0(34)\).

Proof.

  1. \(\Phi_N(j,j_N)=0\) for \(N\in\{2,17\}\). The function \(g=\Phi_N(j,j_N)\) is a modular function for \(\Gamma_0(N)\) that is holomorphic on \(\mathfrak H\). In terms of the local parameter \(q\) at the cusp \(\infty\), \(j\) has a simple pole and \(j_N\) a pole of order \(N\); by the Fricke involution in (M1), \(j\) has a pole of order \(N\) and \(j_N\) a simple pole at the cusp \(0\). So a monomial \(j^aj_N^b\) with \(a,b\le N+1\) has a pole of order at most \((N+1)^2\) at each cusp. If \(g\) were nonzero and vanished at \(\infty\), all its poles would be at the cusp \(0\), and \(g\) would have at most \((N+1)^2\) zeros, counted with multiplicity. But by (C0), \(g\) vanishes at \(\infty\) to order at least \(72>9\) for \(N=2\) and at least \(402>324\) for \(N=17\). Hence \(g=0\).

  2. \(\Q(X_0(34))=\Q(j,j_2,j_{17})\), of degree \(54\) over \(\Q(j)\). For \(\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\) we have \(j_d\circ\gamma=j_d\) if and only if \(\gamma\in\Gamma_0(d)\): with \(\delta=\mathrm{diag}(d,1)\) we have \(j_d\circ\gamma=j\circ(\delta\gamma\delta^{-1})\circ\delta\), and this equals \(j\circ\delta\) exactly when \(\delta\gamma\delta^{-1}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\). (If \(j\circ M=j\) for some \(M\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Q)\), then \(\mathfrak H\) is the union of the countably many closed sets \(\{z:\gamma^{-1}Mz=z\}\), \(\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\), so some \(\gamma^{-1}M\) fixes a nonempty open set and equals \(\pm1\); hence \(M=\pm\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\).) Hence the pairs \((j_2\circ\gamma,j_{17}\circ\gamma)\), for \(\gamma\) in a set of representatives of \(\Gamma_0(34)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\), are \(54\) distinct pairs, and for all but finitely many \(c\in\Q\) the \(54\) functions \(\vartheta\circ\gamma\), where \(\vartheta=j_2+c\,j_{17}\), are distinct. They are roots of the minimal polynomial of \(\vartheta\) over \(\mathbb C(j)\), whose coefficients are invariant under \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\). So \([\mathbb C(j,j_2,j_{17}):\mathbb C(j)]\ge54\); as this field is contained in \(\mathbb C(j,j_{34})\), of degree \(54\) by (M1), the two fields are equal.

    Elements of \(\Q((q))\) that are linearly independent over \(\Q(j)\) remain linearly independent over \(\mathbb C(j)\): in a relation with coefficients in \(\mathbb C[j]\), expand the complex coefficients in a basis of a finite-dimensional \(\Q\)-vector space containing them. So \([M:\Q(j)]=[\mathbb C(j)M:\mathbb C(j)]\) for every field \(\Q(j)\subseteq M\subset\Q((q))\) that is finite over \(\Q(j)\). The functions \(j_2,j_{17},j_{34}\) are algebraic over \(\Q(j)\): the first two by step 1, and \(j_{34}\) is a root of \(\Phi_{17}(j_2,v)\). Therefore the fields \(\Q(j,j_{34})\), \(\Q(j,j_2,j_{17})\) and \(\Q(j,j_2,j_{17},j_{34})\) all have degree \(54\) over \(\Q(j)\), and they coincide.

  3. The isomorphism \(\Theta\). The \(\Q(j)\)-algebra

    \[ \mathcal A=\Q(j)[v_2,v_{17}]/\bigl(\Phi_2(j,v_2),\ \Phi_{17}(j,v_{17})\bigr) \]

    has dimension \(3\cdot18=54\) by (C0), and by step 1 it maps onto \(\Q(j,j_2,j_{17})\) via \(v_d\mapsto j_d\). By step 2 this map is an isomorphism, so \(\mathcal A\) is a field, isomorphic to \(\Q(X_0(34))\). By (C2a) and (C2c), the assignment

    \[ j\mapsto j_{\mathcal X},\qquad v_2\mapsto j_{\mathcal X}\circ\omega_2,\qquad v_{17}\mapsto j_{\mathcal X}\circ\omega_{17} \]

    defines a homomorphism from \(\mathcal A\) to the function field \(\Q(\mathcal X)\). For (C2c), note that \(h=\Phi_{17}(j_{\mathcal X},j_{\mathcal X}\circ\omega_{17})\) has a divisor of poles of degree at most \(18\cdot54+18\cdot54=1944\), and that it vanishes at \(2004\) points; so \(h=0\). The homomorphism is injective, and \([\Q(\mathcal X):\Q(j_{\mathcal X})]=54\)\({}=[\mathcal A:\Q(j)]\), so it is an isomorphism \(\Q(X_0(34))\to\Q(\mathcal X)\). It corresponds to an isomorphism \(\Theta:\mathcal X\to X_0(34)\) over \(\Q\) with \(j\circ\Theta=j_{\mathcal X}\) and \(j_2\circ\Theta=j_{\mathcal X}\circ\omega_2\).

  4. \(\Theta\circ\omega_2=w_2\circ\Theta\). Put \(w=\Theta\circ\omega_2\circ\Theta^{-1}\). Then \(j\circ w=j_2=j\circ w_2\) by step 3 and (M3), so the automorphism \(u=w\circ w_2^{-1}\) of \(X_0(34)\) satisfies \(j\circ u=j\). Thus \(u\) permutes the poles of \(j\) and preserves their orders. By (C4), transported by \(\Theta\), these orders are \(34,17,2,1\); their sum is \(54=\deg j\), so these are all the poles. Hence \(u\) fixes the simple pole \(P_\infty\) of \(j\). The function \(1/j\) is a uniformizer at \(P_\infty\) and is fixed by \(u\); so \(u\) acts trivially on the completed local ring at \(P_\infty\), hence on the function field, and \(u=1\). ∎

Proposition 9. Let \(K\) be a quadratic field with nontrivial automorphism \(\sigma\), and let \(x\in X_0(34)(K)\) be a non-cuspidal point with \(w_2(x)=x^\sigma\). Then \(K=\Q(\sqrt{-15})\).

Proof. Let \(x'=\Theta^{-1}(x)\in\mathcal X(K)\), with \(\Theta\) as in Lemma 8; it is not a pole of \(j_{\mathcal X}\), and \(\omega_2(x')=x'^\sigma\). Then \(\pi(x')^\sigma=\pi(x'^\sigma)\)\({}=\pi(\omega_2(x'))=\pi(x')\), so \(\pi(x')\) is one of the four rational points of \(\mathcal Y\) of (C3). By (C4), the fibres of \(\pi\) over two of them consist of poles of \(j_{\mathcal X}\). So \(x'\) lies in one of the other two fibres, each of which is a single closed point with residue field \(\Q(\sqrt{-15})\). Hence \(\Q(\sqrt{-15})\subseteq K\), and \(K=\Q(\sqrt{-15})\). ∎

Conversely, by (C4\('\)) the four points over \(\Q(\sqrt{-15})\) do satisfy \(w_2(x)=x^\sigma\). So the fact that \(K\) is real is essential in Section 6. As a by-product, the rational points of \(X_0(34)\) are its four cusps.

A check by hand of (C3) and (C4). Put \(a=x+z\) and \(b=x-z\). Then \(\omega_2\) becomes \((a:b:y)\mapsto(-a:b:y)\), and the equation of \(\mathcal X\) becomes

\[ 8f=a^4+(8b^2+6y^2)\,a^2-(b^2+2y^2)(b^2+4y^2), \]

a quadratic polynomial in \(a^2\) with discriminant \(4W^2\), where \(W^2=17b^4+30b^2y^2+17y^4\). The map \((a:b:y)\)\({}\mapsto(b:y:a^2+4b^2+3y^2)\) identifies the quotient \(\mathcal X/\omega_2\) with this genus-\(1\) curve. It has the four rational points \((b:y:W)=(\pm1:1:\pm8)\), and by (C3) no others. Above a rational point, \(a^2=W-4b^2-3y^2\), which is \(1\) for \(W=8\) and \(-15\) for \(W=-8\). The value \(1\) gives the four rational points of (C4), and the value \(-15\) gives the points over \(\Q(\sqrt{-15})\).

8. Proof of the Theorem and consequence for the census

Proof of the Theorem. Let \(A/\Q\) be a geometrically split abelian surface. By Section 2 it is of type (N), (CM) or (S). In types (N) and (CM), \(A(\Q)\) has no point of order \(17\) by Section 3 and Section 4. In type (S) the group \(\Gamma\) is one of the nine groups of Section 2: the groups \(C_1\) and \(C_2\) are treated in Section 3, the groups \(C_3,C_6,V_4,D_6,D_8,D_{12}\) in Section 5, and \(C_4\) in Section 6, using Proposition 9. ∎

Proof of the Corollary. The Jacobian of a curve of genus \(2\) over \(\Q\) is an abelian surface over \(\Q\). ∎

The group [17] is impossible in the class “geometrically split, simple over \(\Q\)”. It was already impossible in the class “split over \(\Q\)” by Mazur's theorem (prime torsion obstruction). Hence no geometrically split genus-\(2\) Jacobian over \(\Q\) has a rational point of order \(17\), and the combined geometrically split cell of [17], whose grade was open (“\(\infty\)?” on the census), becomes impossible (\(\emptyset\)).

The Theorem applies to every group containing a point of order \(17\). Among the census groups these are [17] and [34]. The latter was already excluded in both geometrically split classes by the proof for orders 33 and 34.

The argument places no restriction on the geometric splitting degree, the field of definition of the geometric endomorphisms, or the integral endomorphism ring, and it does not use a principal polarization.

9. Remarks

  1. Fields of degree \(4\). By Derickx, Kamienny, Stein and Stoll [DKSS, Theorem 1.2], \(S(4)=\mathrm{Primes}(17)\): there are elliptic curves over quartic fields with a point of order \(17\). This is why the argument of Section 3 cannot be used when \([L:\Q]\ge4\). For \(\Gamma=C_3\), where \([L:\Q]=3\), it could be used, since \(S(3)=\mathrm{Primes}(13)\) by Parent's theorem, as quoted in [DKSS, Section 1]; we treat \(C_3\) together with \(C_6\) in Section 5 instead.

  2. Quadratic points of \(X_0(34)\). Ozman and Siksek [OS] determined all quadratic points of \(X_0(34)\) (their Main Theorem and Table 8.1). The non-cuspidal ones are defined over \(\Q(\sqrt{-1})\), \(\Q(\sqrt{-2})\) and \(\Q(\sqrt{-15})\). Those over \(\Q(\sqrt{-15})\) are the points \(P_5\), \(P_6\) of their table and the conjugates; they have no CM, the involution \(w_2\) maps each of them to its conjugate, and their \(j\)-invariants \((2041\theta+11779)/8\) and \((-53184785340479\,\theta\)\({}-7319387769191)\)\({}/34359738368\), with \(\theta^2=-15\), have the minimal polynomials of (C4). Since none of the three fields is real, their theorem gives a second proof of the last step of Section 6. The proof above does not depend on it.

  3. Potentially good reduction. By Clark [Cla], the order of the rational torsion subgroup of an abelian surface over \(\Q\) with potentially good reduction everywhere belongs to \(\{1,\ldots,16,18,19,20,22,24,\)\({}25,28,30,36,48,60,72\}\), which contains no multiple of \(17\). Proposition 3 is the part of this statement that is needed here; it only uses the reduction at \(2\).

  4. Relation to the earlier proofs. The Theorem supersedes the last sentence of the proof for orders 33 and 34, which left rational \(17\)-torsion open. The lemmas of Section 5 and the construction of Section 6 are the analogues for \(p=17\) of those in the proof for order 13; they are given here in full. For \(p=17\) the non-residues \(-3\) and \(-1/3\) dispose of the groups \(C_3\) and \(C_6\) together with the dihedral groups, so that \(C_4\) is the only group which requires a modular curve; and, in contrast to order \(13\), neither a principal polarization nor simplicity over \(\Q\) is needed. The construction of the curve \(E_c\) in Section 6 is a special case of Elkies's reduction of quadratic \(K\)-curves (Elkies [Elk]).
