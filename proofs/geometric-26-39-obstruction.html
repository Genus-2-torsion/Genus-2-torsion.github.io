Excluding torsion of orders 26 and 39

21 October 2026 · Geometric splitting obstruction

Theorem. Let \(A/\Q\) be a geometrically split abelian surface. Then \(A(\Q)\) contains no point of order \(26\) and no point of order \(39\).

In particular, none of [26], [2,26] and [39] occurs as the rational torsion subgroup of a geometrically split genus-\(2\) Jacobian over \(\Q\).

Here “geometrically split” means that \(A_{\overline{\Q}}\) is isogenous to a product of two elliptic curves. There is no restriction on the degree of this isogeny, on the field of definition of the geometric endomorphisms, or on the integral endomorphism ring, and the theorem does not require a principal polarization. Along the way we prove two statements about non-CM geometric squares whose endomorphism field is cyclic of degree \(6\) (Proposition 9.1 and Lemma 10.1); they need no torsion hypothesis. One step uses a Magma computation on the modular curve \(X_0(39)\), which is published below together with its log.

1. Inputs

We use the following results on torsion points of elliptic curves.

  1. Mazur, Theorem (7′): if an elliptic curve over \(\Q\) has a rational point of order \(m\), then \(m\le10\) or \(m=12\). In particular, no elliptic curve over \(\Q\) has a rational point of order \(13\).

  2. Kenku–Momose, Theorem (0.1): let \(N\) be a product of powers of \(2,3,5,7,11,13\) and let \(m\mid N\). If \(X_1(m,N)\) has positive genus and \((m,N)\) is not one of \((1,11)\), \((1,13)\), \((1,14)\), \((1,15)\), \((1,16)\), \((1,18)\), \((2,10)\), \((2,12)\), then \(Y_1(m,N)(k)=\emptyset\) for every quadratic field \(k\). Taking \((m,N)=(1,26)\) and \((1,39)\) (the curves \(X_1(26)\) and \(X_1(39)\) have genus \(10\) and \(33\)), no elliptic curve over a quadratic field has a rational point of order \(26\) or \(39\).

  3. Derickx–Etropolski–van Hoeij–Morrow–Zureick-Brown, Theorem A: if \(E\) is an elliptic curve over a cubic field \(K\), then \(E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) is \(\Z/N\) with \(N\in\{1,\ldots,16,18,20,21\}\), or \(\Z/2\times\Z/2N\) with \(1\le N\le7\). None of these groups has an element of order \(26\) or \(39\).

  4. Derickx–Najman, Theorem 1.1: over quartic fields the torsion groups of elliptic curves are \(\Z/n\) with \(n=1,\ldots,18,20,21,22,24\), and \(\Z/2\times\Z/2n\) \((n\le9)\), \(\Z/3\times\Z/3n\) \((n\le3)\), \(\Z/4\times\Z/4n\) \((n\le2)\), \(\Z/5\times\Z/5\), \(\Z/6\times\Z/6\). None of them has an element of order \(26\) or \(39\).

Together, these give:

\[ \text{if } [K:\Q]\le 4, \text{ no elliptic curve over } K \text{ has a } K\text{-rational point of order } 26 \text{ or } 39. \tag{1.1} \]

We also use the following.

  • Faltings, §5, Satz 3, Satz 4 and Korollar 1: for abelian varieties \(X,Y\) over a number field \(K\) and a prime \(\ell\), the representation of \(G_K=\Gal(\overline K/K)\) on \(V_\ell(X)=T_\ell(X)\otimes\Q_\ell\) is semisimple, and the natural map

    \[ \Hom_K(X,Y)\otimes\Z_\ell\longrightarrow\Hom_{G_K}(T_\ell X,T_\ell Y) \]

    is an isomorphism.

  • The criterion of Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich, Serre–Tate, §1, Theorem 1: an abelian variety over a finite extension \(F\) of \(\Q_p\) has good reduction if and only if the inertia group \(I_F\) acts trivially on \(T_\ell\) for one (equivalently, every) prime \(\ell\ne p\). Moreover, an elliptic curve with complex multiplication over a number field has potentially good reduction at every prime (Serre–Tate, §5, Theorem 6(a)).

  • Weil's theorem: if \(\bar A\) is an abelian surface over \(\F_q\), then \(\#\bar A(\F_q)=f(1)\), where \(f\in\Z[T]\) is the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius; it is monic of degree \(4\), and all its complex roots have absolute value \(\sqrt q\).

  • Lattice independence (Brauer–Nesbitt): if a compact group acts continuously on a finite-dimensional \(\Q_p\)-vector space, the reductions modulo \(p\) of any two stable lattices have isomorphic semisimplifications. This applies to isomorphic representations after transporting a lattice through the isomorphism. We also use the Skolem–Noether theorem and Clifford's theorem; the latter is proved where it is used.

2. Four types and two elementary lemmas

For a prime \(p\) let \(T_p(A)\) be the Tate module and \(V_p(A)=T_p(A)\otimes\Q_p\), with their actions of \(G_{\Q}=\Gal(\overline\Q/\Q)\). For a stable lattice \(T'\subset V_p(A)\), the module \(T'/pT'\) is its residual representation, and \((\cdot)^{\mathrm{ss}}\) denotes semisimplification. By lattice independence, \(A[p]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) depends only on the \(\Q\)-isogeny class of \(A\). A rational point of order \(p\) spans a trivial submodule of \(A[p]=T_p(A)/pT_p(A)\); hence the residual representation of every \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice in \(V_p(A)\) has a trivial composition factor. We write \(\bar\chi_p\) for the mod-\(p\) cyclotomic character.

A geometrically split abelian surface \(A/\Q\) is of exactly one of the following types.

  • (Q) \(A\) is not simple over \(\Q\).
  • (N) \(A\) is simple over \(\Q\), and \(A_{\overline\Q}\sim E_1\times E_2\) with \(E_1,E_2\) not isogenous.
  • (CM) \(A\) is simple over \(\Q\), and \(A_{\overline\Q}\sim E^2\) with \(E\) having complex multiplication.
  • (S) \(A\) is simple over \(\Q\), and \(A_{\overline\Q}\sim E^2\) with \(E\) without complex multiplication.

Suppose from now on that \(P\in A(\Q)\) has order \(N\in\{26,39\}\). Write \(N=13s\) with \(s\in\{2,3\}\), and put \(P_{13}=sP\) and \(P_s=13P\); these are rational points of orders \(13\) and \(s\).

Lemma 2.1 (coprime torsion). Let \(K\) be a number field and \(m,n\) coprime positive integers. Let \(E_m,E_n\) be \(K\)-isogenous elliptic curves over \(K\) such that \(E_m(K)\) has a point of order \(m\) and \(E_n(K)\) has a point of order \(n\). Then some elliptic curve over \(K\) that is \(K\)-isogenous to \(E_m\) has a \(K\)-rational point of order \(mn\).

Proof. Let \(f:E_m\to E_n\) be a \(K\)-isogeny, and let \(\Phi\subset\ker f\) be the subgroup of elements whose order is divisible only by primes dividing \(n\). It is characteristic in \(\ker f\), hence \(G_K\)-stable. Factor \(f\) as

\[ E_m\xrightarrow{\ a\ }E'=E_m/\Phi\xrightarrow{\ b\ }E_n . \]

The degree of \(a\) is prime to \(m\), so \(a\) maps the given point of order \(m\) to a \(K\)-point of order \(m\) on \(E'\). The degree of \(b\) is prime to \(n\). Since \(b\circ\hat b=[\deg b]\), the dual isogeny \(\hat b\) is injective on \(E_n[n]\), so it maps the given point of order \(n\) to a \(K\)-point of order \(n\) on \(E'\). The sum of the two points has order \(mn\). ∎

Lemma 2.2. Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(0\), let \(X\) be an abelian surface over \(k\), let \(E\subset X\) be an elliptic curve defined over \(k\), and put \(F=X/E\). If \(Q\in X(k)\) has prime order \(q\), then either \(Q\in E(k)\), or the image of \(Q\) in \(F(k)\) has order \(q\).

Proof. The image of \(Q\) is killed by \(q\), and it is zero exactly when \(Q\in E\). ∎

3. Surfaces that are not simple over Q, and nonisogenous factors

Type (Q). If \(A\) is not simple over \(\Q\), it contains an elliptic curve \(E\) defined over \(\Q\), and \(A/E\) is an elliptic curve over \(\Q\). By Lemma 2.2 with \(k=\Q\), the point \(P_{13}\) gives a rational point of order \(13\) on \(E\) or on \(A/E\). This contradicts Mazur's theorem.

Type (N). Suppose \(A_{\overline\Q}\sim E_1\times E_2\) with \(E_1\not\sim E_2\). For each \(i\), all nonzero homomorphisms \(E_i\to A_{\overline\Q}\) have the same image: \(\Hom(E_i,A_{\overline\Q})\otimes\Q\) is a vector space of dimension one over \(\End^0(E_i)\), because \(\Hom(E_1,E_2)=0\). Every elliptic curve in \(A_{\overline\Q}\) is isogenous to \(E_1\) or to \(E_2\). Hence \(A_{\overline\Q}\) has exactly two elliptic subvarieties. The group \(G_{\Q}\) permutes them. If it fixed them, they would be defined over \(\Q\), and \(A\) would not be simple over \(\Q\). Hence they are exchanged, and they are defined over a quadratic field \(K\). Write them as \(E\) and \(E^\sigma\), where \(\sigma\) generates \(\Gal(K/\Q)\).

Put \(F=A_K/E\). Since \(E\cap E^\sigma\) is finite and \(E+E^\sigma=A\), the quotient map restricts to a \(K\)-isogeny \(E^\sigma\to F\); hence \(F^\sigma\) is \(K\)-isogenous to \(E\). Apply Lemma 2.2 over \(K\) to \(P_{13}\) and to \(P_s\). If the point lands in \(F(K)\), applying \(\sigma\) gives a point of the same order in \(F^\sigma(K)\). Thus the \(K\)-isogeny class of \(E\) contains curves with \(K\)-points of orders \(13\) and \(s\). Lemma 2.1 gives an elliptic curve over the quadratic field \(K\) with a \(K\)-point of order \(N\), contradicting (1.1).

4. The CM case: a local argument

Lemma 4.1. Let \(p\) be a prime and \(Y/\Q_p\) an abelian variety with potentially good reduction. There is an abelian variety \(\bar Y\) over \(\F_p\), of the same dimension, such that \(Y(\Q_p)[M]\) embeds into \(\bar Y(\F_p)\) for every integer \(M\) prime to \(p\).

Proof. This is the argument of Clark, §2.1 and of Clark–Xarles, Main Theorem (i) and §2.4, who quote the first step from Serre–Tate, p. 498. We include it for completeness.

A totally ramified extension. Fix a prime \(\ell\ne p\), let \(\rho:G_{\Q_p}\to\GL(T_\ell Y)\) be the Galois representation, and let \(I\subset G_{\Q_p}\) be the inertia group. Let \(F'/\Q_p\) be a finite extension over which \(Y\) has good reduction. By the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion, \(I\cap G_{F'}\) acts trivially on \(T_\ell Y\). It has finite index in \(I\), so \(\rho(I)\) is finite. The group \(N_0=I\cap\ker\rho\) is closed and normal in \(G_{\Q_p}\). Put \(\mathcal G=G_{\Q_p}/N_0\). Then \(I/N_0\) is a finite normal subgroup of \(\mathcal G\), and \(\mathcal G/(I/N_0)\simeq G_{\Q_p}/I\simeq\widehat\Z\). Since \(\widehat\Z\) is a free profinite group, the projection \(\mathcal G\to\widehat\Z\) has a continuous section. Let \(S\) be its image. Then \(S\cap(I/N_0)=1\) and \(S\cdot(I/N_0)=\mathcal G\). Let \(F\) be the fixed field of the preimage of \(S\) in \(G_{\Q_p}\). Then \([F:\Q_p]=\#(I/N_0)\) is finite. The field \(F\cap\Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}\) is fixed by \(G_F\) and by \(I\), whose images generate \(\mathcal G\). Hence \(F\cap\Q_p^{\mathrm{nr}}=\Q_p\), so \(F/\Q_p\) is totally ramified and has residue field \(\F_p\). The inertia group \(G_F\cap I\) maps into \(S\cap(I/N_0)=1\), so it lies in \(\ker\rho\). By the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion, \(Y_F\) has good reduction.

Injectivity. Let \(\mathcal Y\) be the abelian scheme over the ring of integers \(\mathcal O_F\) with generic fibre \(Y_F\), and let \(\bar Y\) be its special fibre, an abelian variety over \(\F_p\). For \(M\) prime to \(p\), the group scheme \(\mathcal Y[M]\) is finite étale over the henselian local ring \(\mathcal O_F\). Hence reduction \(\mathcal Y[M](\mathcal O_F)\to\bar Y[M](\F_p)\) is injective. By properness, \(Y(F)[M]=\mathcal Y[M](\mathcal O_F)\). Thus \(Y(\Q_p)[M]\subset Y(F)[M]\) embeds into \(\bar Y(\F_p)\). ∎

Proposition 4.2. If \(A_{\overline\Q}\) is isogenous to \(E^2\) for an elliptic curve \(E\) with complex multiplication, then \(A(\Q)\) has no point of order \(26\) or \(39\).

Proof. Let \(p\) be a prime. Let \(M\) be a number field over which \(E\) and an isogeny \(A_M\to E_M^2\) are defined, and let \(v\) be a place of \(M\) above \(p\). Since \(E\) has potentially good reduction at \(v\), there is a finite extension \(F'\) of \(M_v\) over which \(E\) has good reduction. Then \(V_\ell(A)\simeq V_\ell(E)^2\) as representations of \(G_{F'}\) for \(\ell\ne p\), so the inertia group of \(F'\) acts trivially on \(T_\ell(A)\). By the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion, \(A\) has good reduction over \(F'\). Thus \(A_{\Q_p}\) has potentially good reduction, and Lemma 4.1 applies to it, giving an abelian surface \(\bar A\) over \(\F_p\).

Order 39. Take \(p=2\). Since \(39\) is odd, \(\Z/39\) embeds into \(\bar A(\F_2)\), so \(\#\bar A(\F_2)\ge39\). But Weil's theorem gives \(\#\bar A(\F_2)\le(1+\sqrt2)^4\) \(=17+12\sqrt2\lt 34\), a contradiction.

Order 26. Take \(p=3\). Then \(\Z/26\) embeds into \(\bar A(\F_3)\), so \(26\) divides \(\#\bar A(\F_3)\), and \(\#\bar A(\F_3)\le(1+\sqrt3)^4\) \(=28+16\sqrt3\lt 56\). Hence \(\#\bar A(\F_3)\in\{26,52\}\). Write the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius as

\[ f(T)=T^4+aT^3+bT^2+3aT+9,\qquad a,b\in\Z . \]

Then \(f(T)=T^2h(T+3/T)\) with \(h(X)=X^2+aX+(b-6)\). If \(c\) is a root of \(h\), the roots \(\pi\) of \(T^2-cT+3\) are roots of \(f\), so \(|\pi|=\sqrt3\) and \(c=\pi+3/\pi=\pi+\bar\pi\) is real with \(|c|\le2\sqrt3\). Let \(c_1,c_2\) be the roots of \(h\). Then \(a=-(c_1+c_2)\) and \(b=6+c_1c_2\). The bounds \(|c_i|\le2\sqrt3\), the inequality \(c_1c_2\le\big((c_1+c_2)/2\big)^2\) and the inequalities \((2\sqrt3\mp c_1)(2\sqrt3\mp c_2)\ge0\) give

\[ |a|\le4\sqrt3,\qquad 2\sqrt3\,|a|-6\ \le\ b\ \le\ \frac{a^2}{4}+6. \tag{4.1} \]

We have \(\#\bar A(\F_3)=f(1)=10+4a+b\).

  • If \(f(1)=26\), then \(b=16-4a\). The upper bound in (4.1) gives \(a^2+16a-40\ge0\). Together with \(|a|\le4\sqrt3\) this forces \(a\ge-8+\sqrt{104}\), so \(a\ge3\). The lower bound then reads \((4+2\sqrt3)\,a\le22\), so \(a\le2\). This is a contradiction.

  • If \(f(1)=52\), then \(b=42-4a\). The upper bound gives \(a^2+16a-144\ge0\). With \(|a|\le4\sqrt3\) this forces \(a\ge-8+\sqrt{208}\gt 6\), so \(a\ge7\gt 4\sqrt3\). This is a contradiction. ∎

The supplementary script confirms these two computations by listing every value of \(f(1)\) allowed by the condition on the roots of \(h\): over \(\F_3\) the values \(26\) and \(52\) do not occur, and over \(\F_2\) all values are at most \(25\). As an independent check, Clark, Theorem 5 (the same list is Clark–Xarles, Theorem 1.4) shows that the torsion order of an abelian surface over \(\Q\) with everywhere potentially good reduction lies in

\[ \{1,\ldots,16,18,19,20,22,24,25,28,30,36,48,60,72\}; \]

no element of this set is divisible by \(26\) or \(39\).

5. The non-CM square case: the endomorphism field

From now on, \(A\) has type (S). Put

\[ D=\End^0(A_{\overline\Q})=\End(A_{\overline\Q})\otimes\Q\simeq M_2(\Q). \]

Let \(L\) be the smallest field over which all geometric endomorphisms of \(A\) are defined. It is a finite Galois extension of \(\Q\). Put \(\Gamma=\Gal(L/\Q)\). The group \(G_{\Q}\) acts on \(D\) through \(\Gamma\), and \(\Gamma\) acts faithfully. By Skolem–Noether, each \(g\in\Gamma\) acts as \(\Ad(R_g)\) for some \(R_g\in\GL_2(\Q)\), unique up to \(\Q^\times\). This embeds \(\Gamma\) into \(\PGL_2(\Q)\); we write \(g=[R_g]\). Note that \(\End^0_{\Q}(A)=D^{\Gamma}\), and that \(\Gamma\ne1\): otherwise every elliptic subvariety of \(A_{\overline\Q}\) would be defined over \(\Q\) (Lemma 5.2 below), contradicting simplicity over \(\Q\).

Lemma 5.1 (finite subgroups of \(\PGL_2(\Q)\)). \(\Gamma\) is one of \(C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4,C_6\), \(D_4\simeq C_2\times C_2\), \(D_6,D_8,D_{12}\), where \(C_n\) is cyclic of order \(n\) and the subscript of \(D\) is the group order. If \([R]\in\PGL_2(\Q)\) has order \(n\in\{3,4,6\}\), then \(\operatorname{tr}R\ne0\), and the unique representative with trace \(1\) has characteristic polynomial \(X^2-X+1/c\), where \(c=1,2,3\) for \(n=3,4,6\); its discriminant is \(-3,-1,-1/3\), respectively. If \([R]\) has order \(2\), then \(\operatorname{tr}R=0\) and \(R^2\in\Q^\times\).

Proof. A finite subgroup of \(\PGL_2(\R)\) fixes a point of the hyperbolic plane, and its action on the tangent plane identifies it with a finite subgroup of \(O(2)\). Hence it is cyclic or dihedral. If \([R]\) has order \(n\gt2\), the ratio of the eigenvalues of \(R\) is a primitive \(n\)-th root of unity \(\zeta\), and

\[ \frac{(\operatorname{tr}R)^2}{\det R}=2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\in\Q, \]

which forces \(n\in\{3,4,6\}\), with value \(c=1,2,3\). Normalizing \(\operatorname{tr}R=1\) gives \(\det R=1/c\). If \([R]\) has order \(2\), then \(R^2\) is a scalar while \(R\) is not, so the characteristic polynomial of \(R\) is \(X^2-R^2\). ∎

In a dihedral group \(D_{2n}\) with \(n\in\{3,4,6\}\), let \(r\) generate the rotation subgroup and let \(R\) be its trace-one representative. A reflection \(t\) satisfies \(trt^{-1}=r^{-1}\). Since \(R^{-1}=(1-R)/\det R\), the trace-one representative of \(r^{-1}\) is \(1-R\). Conjugation preserves the trace, so \(\Ad(R_t)(R)=1-R\).

Lemma 5.2. Every elliptic curve \(E\subset A_{\overline\Q}\) is defined over \(L\), and \(A_L/E\) is \(L\)-isogenous to \(E\).

Proof. The curve \(E\) is the image of \(n\epsilon\) for a rank-one idempotent \(\epsilon\in D\) and a suitable integer \(n\ge1\). This endomorphism is defined over \(L\), so \(E\) is defined over \(L\). Let \(E'\) be the image of \(n(1-\epsilon)\). Then \(E+E'=A\) and \(E\cap E'\) is finite, so the quotient map restricts to an \(L\)-isogeny \(E'\to A_L/E\). Since \(D=M_2(\Q)\), we have \(\epsilon D(1-\epsilon)\ne0\), so there is a nonzero homomorphism between \(E'\) and \(E\). It is defined over \(L\). Hence \(E\sim_L E'\sim_L A_L/E\). ∎

6. Three lemmas on residual representations

In this section \(A\) has type (S), \(p\) is an odd prime, \(T=T_p(A)\) and \(V=V_p(A)\). The algebra \(D_p=D\otimes\Q_p\simeq M_2(\Q_p)\) acts on \(V\), and for \(g\in G_{\Q}\), \(\varphi\in D_p\), \(v\in V\) we have \(g(\varphi v)=g(\varphi)\,g(v)\). As a module over \(M_2(\Q_p)\), \(V\) is the sum of two copies of the standard module \(\Q_p^2\).

Lemma 6.1 (polarization). Let \(T'\subset V\) be a \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice such that every composition factor of \((T'/pT')|_{G_L}\) is trivial. Then \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subset L\).

Proof. Choose a polarization of \(A\) defined over \(\Q\); every abelian variety over \(\Q\) has one. Its Weil pairing is a nondegenerate alternating \(G_{\Q}\)-equivariant pairing \(V\times V\to\Q_p(1)\), so \(V\simeq V^*\otimes\Q_p(1)\). Under this isomorphism the lattice \(\Hom(T',\Z_p(1))\) becomes a \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice in \(V\) whose reduction is \((T'/pT')^*\otimes\bar\chi_p\). Restricted to \(G_L\), its composition factors are four copies of \(\bar\chi_p|_{G_L}\). Lattice independence over \(G_L\) gives \(\bar\chi_p|_{G_L}=1\), that is, \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subset L\). The degree of the polarization plays no role. ∎

Lemma 6.2 (unramified order). Let \(\mathcal B\subset D_p\) be a \(\Z_p\)-order isomorphic to the ring of integers of the unramified quadratic extension of \(\Q_p\). Suppose that \(\Ad(R_g)(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B\) for every \(g\in\Gamma\), and that some \(g_0\in\Gamma\) is represented by a unit \(R_{g_0}\in\mathcal B^\times\) whose reduction lies in \(\mathcal B/p\mathcal B\setminus\F_p\simeq\F_{p^2}\setminus\F_p\). If \(A(\Q)[p]\ne0\), then \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subset L\).

Proof.

  1. A stable maximal order. Identify \(D_p\) with \(\End_{\Q_p}(\Q_p^2)\). The \(\mathcal B\)-stable lattices in \(\Q_p^2\) are the fractional ideals of the discrete valuation ring \(\mathcal B\) times a fixed vector, so they form a single homothety class \([\Lambda]\). For \(g\in\Gamma\), the lattice \(R_g\Lambda\) is stable under \(R_g\mathcal BR_g^{-1}=\mathcal B\), so it lies in \([\Lambda]\). Hence the maximal order \(\mathcal R=\End_{\Z_p}(\Lambda)\simeq M_2(\Z_p)\) is \(\Gamma\)-stable, and we may rescale each \(R_g\) so that \(R_g\in\GL(\Lambda)\).

  2. A tensor decomposition. Put \(T'=\mathcal R\,T\). It is a lattice, and it is \(G_{\Q}\)-stable because \(g(xt)=g(x)g(t)\) with \(g(x)\in\mathcal R\). By Morita theory, \(T'\simeq\Lambda\otimes_{\Z_p}W_0\), with \(\mathcal R\) acting on the first factor and \(W_0=e_{11}T'\) free of rank \(2\).

  3. Linear actions on the factors. Let \(\widetilde G\) be the group of pairs \((g,M)\) with \(g\in G_{\Q}\), \(M\in\GL(\Lambda)\) and \(\Ad(M)=g\) on \(\mathcal R\). It surjects onto \(G_{\Q}\) with kernel \(\Z_p^\times\). For \((g,M)\in\widetilde G\), the operator \((M\otimes1)^{-1}g\) on \(T'\) commutes with \(\mathcal R\), so it equals \(1\otimes n\) for a unique \(n\in\GL(W_0)\). Thus \(\widetilde G\) acts linearly on \(\Lambda\) and on \(W_0\), and the tensor product of these actions is the Galois action. Reducing modulo \(p\), we get \(\bar V:=T'/pT'\simeq U\otimes W\) with \(U=\Lambda/p\Lambda\) and \(W=W_0/pW_0\). The group \(\widetilde G\) acts on \(\bar V\) through \(G_{\Q}\), so its submodules and composition factors are the same for \(\widetilde G\) and for \(G_{\Q}\).

  4. \(U\) is irreducible. Let \(g\in G_{\Q}\) map to \(g_0\). Any \(M\) with \((g,M)\in\widetilde G\) equals \(\lambda R_{g_0}\) with \(\lambda\in\Z_p^\times\). The \(\F_p\)-vector space \(U\) is a one-dimensional \(\F_{p^2}\)-vector space, and \(M\) acts on it as multiplication by an element of \(\F_{p^2}\setminus\F_p\). This multiplication has no eigenvector over \(\F_p\), so \(U\) has no stable line.

  5. \(W\) is irreducible. By lattice independence, \(\bar V\) has a trivial composition factor. If \(W\) had a stable line \(\ell\), then \(U\otimes\ell\) and \(U\otimes(W/\ell)\) would be twists of \(U\) by characters, hence irreducible of dimension \(2\), and \(\bar V\) would have no one-dimensional composition factor.

  6. \(G_L\) acts trivially on \(\bar V\). For \(h\in G_L\) the action of \(h\) on \(\mathcal R\) is trivial, so \((h,1)\in\widetilde G\). The subgroup \(H=\{(h,1):h\in G_L\}\) is normal in \(\widetilde G\) and acts trivially on \(U\). Clifford's theorem shows that \(W|_H\) is semisimple: if \(S\subset W\) is a simple \(H\)-submodule, the translates \(xS\) for \(x\in\widetilde G\) are simple \(H\)-submodules, and their sum is a nonzero \(\widetilde G\)-submodule, hence equal to \(W\). Lemma 6.3 (split blocks). Let \(\mathcal B=\Z_pe_1\oplus\Z_pe_2\subset D_p\) with orthogonal idempotents \(e_1+e_2=1\) of rank one. Suppose that \(\Ad(R_g)(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B\) for every \(g\in\Gamma\) and that some element of \(\Gamma\) exchanges \(e_1\) and \(e_2\). Let \(K\subset L\) be the fixed field of the index-two subgroup fixing \(e_1\). The group \(G_K\) acts on the line \(e_1D_pe_2\) by a character \(\eta\), which factors through \(\Gal(L/K)\). Suppose that its reduction \(\bar\eta\) is nontrivial. If \(A(\Q)[p]\ne0\), then \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subset L\).

Proof.

  1. Two blocks. Put \(T'=\mathcal BT\), a \(G_{\Q}\)-stable lattice, and \(T_i=e_iT'\), so \(T'=T_1\oplus T_2\). Each \(T_i\) has rank \(2\). The group \(G_K\) preserves each \(T_i\), and every element of \(G_{\Q}\setminus G_K\) exchanges them.

  2. The twist relation. Put \(V_i=T_i\otimes\Q_p\), and let \(X\in e_1D_pe_2\) be nonzero. Then \(X:V_2\to V_1\) is an isomorphism, and \(gXg^{-1}=\eta(g)X\) for \(g\in G_K\). Hence \(V_1\simeq\eta\otimes V_2\) as \(G_K\)-representations. With \(M_i=T_i/pT_i\), lattice independence gives \(M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq\bar\eta\otimes M_2^{\mathrm{ss}}\). This does not require \(X\) to map \(T_2\) onto \(T_1\).

  3. Both blocks have a trivial factor. The rational point of order \(p\) gives a trivial \(G_{\Q}\)-composition factor of \(T'/pT'=M_1\oplus M_2\). Restricting to \(G_K\), one of \(M_1,M_2\) has a trivial \(G_K\)-composition factor. An element of \(G_{\Q}\setminus G_K\) carries \(M_1\) to \(M_2\) and conjugates the \(G_K\)-action, so both have one.

  4. All factors are trivial on \(G_L\). Write \(M_2^{\mathrm{ss}}=\mathbf1\oplus\alpha\). Then \(M_1^{\mathrm{ss}}=\bar\eta\oplus\bar\eta\alpha\) contains \(\mathbf1\), and \(\bar\eta\ne1\), so \(\alpha=\bar\eta^{-1}\). All four composition factors \(\mathbf1,\bar\eta^{-1},\bar\eta,\mathbf1\) are trivial on \(G_L\).

  5. Lemma 6.1 gives \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subset L\). ∎

8. The cyclic sextic case: preliminaries

In Sections 8–10, \(A/\Q\) is any abelian surface of type (S) with \(\Gamma=\langle r\rangle\simeq C_6\); no torsion is assumed. Let \(R\) be the trace-one representative of \(r\), so that \(R^2-R+\tfrac13=0\), and let \(K=L^{\langle r^2\rangle}\) be the quadratic subfield of \(L\). For \(g\in G_{\Q}\) write \(g|_L=r^{k(g)}\) with \(k(g)\in\Z/6\). Then \(g\) acts on \(D\) as \(\Ad(R^{k(g)})\), and \(G_K=\{g:k(g)\text{ even}\}\).

8.1. The field \(L\) is totally real

Fix an embedding \(\overline\Q\subset\C\), and let \(c\in G_{\Q}\) be complex conjugation. Put \(H=H_1(A(\C),\R)\), a real vector space of dimension \(4\). The algebra \(D_\R=D\otimes\R\simeq M_2(\R)\) acts faithfully on \(H\), so \(H\simeq U_\R\otimes W_\R\), with \(D_\R\) acting on \(U_\R=\R^2\) and \(\dim W_\R=2\).

  1. The complex structure \(J_H\) commutes with \(D_\R\), because the endomorphisms are holomorphic. Hence \(J_H=1\otimes J\) with \(J\in\End(W_\R)\) and \(J^2=-1\).

  2. Since \(A\) is defined over \(\Q\), complex conjugation induces an involution \(c_*\) of \(H\) with \(c_*J_H=-J_Hc_*\), and \(c_*\varphi c_*^{-1}=c(\varphi)\) for \(\varphi\in D\). Thus \(c_*\) normalizes \(D_\R\otimes1\). By Skolem–Noether there is \(M\in\GL(U_\R)\) with \(c_*(\varphi\otimes1)c_*^{-1}=M\varphi M^{-1}\otimes1\). Then \((M\otimes1)^{-1}c_*\) commutes with \(D_\R\otimes1\), so \(c_*=M\otimes N\) with \(N\in\GL(W_\R)\).

  3. From \(c_*J_H=-J_Hc_*\) we get \(NJ=-JN\). In a basis with \(J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), this means \(N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}x&y\\y&-x\end{smallmatrix}\right)\), so \(N^2=(x^2+y^2)I\) with \(x^2+y^2\gt0\).

  4. From \(c_*^2=1\) we get \(M^2=\mu^2I\) with \(\mu\gt0\). Hence either \(M\) is a scalar, and \(c\) acts trivially on \(D\), or \(M\) has eigenvalues \(\mu,-\mu\), and \(\det M\lt0\).

  5. On the other hand, \(c\) acts on \(D\) as \(\Ad(R^{k(c)})\). If this action were nontrivial, we would have \(M=\lambda R^{k(c)}\) with \(\lambda\in\R^\times\), so \(\det M=\lambda^2\,3^{-k(c)}\gt0\), a contradiction.

Hence \(c\) acts trivially on \(D\). As \(\Gamma\) acts faithfully, \(c|_L=1\). So \(L\) is totally real, and in particular \(K\) is a real quadratic field.

8.2. Normalization

We have \(\End^0_{\Q}(A)=D^\Gamma=\Q[R]\simeq\Q(\sqrt{-3})\). Put \(u=3R-1\). Then \(u^2-u+1=0\), so \(u\) is a primitive sixth root of unity, and

\[ R=\frac{1+u}{3},\qquad R^2=\frac u3,\qquad u^3=-1. \]

Let \(\mathcal O=\Z[u]\simeq\Z[\zeta_3]\). Choose \(m\ge1\) with \(\psi=mu\in\End_{\Q}(A)\), and let \(A'\subset A^2\) be the image of \((m,\psi):A\to A^2\). It is \(\Q\)-isogenous to \(A\). The endomorphism \(u'(x,y)=(y,y-x)\) of \(A^2\) satisfies \(u'^2-u'+1=0\), and \(u'\circ(m,\psi)\circ[m]=(m,\psi)\circ\psi\), because \(\psi^2=m\psi-m^2\). Hence \(u'(A')\subset A'\), and \(u'\) corresponds to \(u\) under the isogeny \(A\to A'\). Thus \(\mathcal O\subset\End_{\Q}(A')\). The surface \(A'\) is again of type (S) with the same \(D\), \(L\) and \(\Gamma\), and \(A'[p]^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq A[p]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) for every prime \(p\).

8.3. A twist with endomorphism field \(K\)

Define \(\xi(g)=u^{-2k(g)}\in\mu_3\subset\Aut_{\Q}(A')\). This is a continuous homomorphism \(G_{\Q}\to\Aut_{\Q}(A')\), hence a \(1\)-cocycle. Let \(B/\Q\) be the corresponding twist: there is an isomorphism \(f:A'_{\overline\Q}\to B_{\overline\Q}\) with \({}^gf=f\circ\xi(g)\) for all \(g\in G_{\Q}\).

  1. Identify \(\End^0(B_{\overline\Q})\) with \(D\) by \(\varphi\mapsto f\varphi f^{-1}\). The Galois action becomes

    \[ \varphi\longmapsto \xi(g)\,{}^g\varphi\,\xi(g)^{-1}=\Ad\big(u^{-2k(g)}R^{k(g)}\big)\varphi=\Ad\big(S^{k(g)}\big)\varphi,\qquad S=u^{-2}R,\quad S^2=\frac{u^{-4}u}{3}=-\frac13 . \]

  2. Put \(\theta=3S=u^{-2}(1+u)\in\mathcal O\). Then \(\theta^2=-3\).

  3. The elements of \(\mathcal O\) commute with \(\xi(g)\) and with \(R\), so they are fixed by the new action: \(\mathcal O\subset\End_{\Q}(B)\). Since \(S^2\) is a scalar, all geometric endomorphisms of \(B\) are defined over \(K\), and for \(\tau\in G_{\Q}\setminus G_K\) we have \({}^\tau\varphi=\theta\varphi\theta^{-1}\).

  4. \(B_{\overline\Q}\simeq A'_{\overline\Q}\) is isogenous to the square of a non-CM elliptic curve.

  5. On Tate modules, \(f\) identifies \(T_\ell(B)\) with \(T_\ell(A')\) on which \(g\) acts by \(x\mapsto\xi(g)\,g(x)\), since \(g(f(x))={}^gf(g(x))=f(\xi(g)g(x))\).

Lemma 8.4. Let \(\epsilon\in D\) be a rank-one idempotent and \(n\ge1\) an integer with \(n\epsilon\in\End(B_{\overline\Q})\).     \[ \Phi_\tau=\iota_1^{-1}\circ\tau_B\circ\iota_1\circ\tau_*^{-1}:\ T_{13}(E_1^\tau)\longrightarrow T_{13}(E_1), \]

10. The cyclic sextic case at 3

Lemma 10.1. Let \(A/\Q\) be of type (S) with \(\Gamma\simeq C_6\). Then \(A[3]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) has no trivial constituent. In particular, \(A(\Q)[3]=0\).

    \[ \theta T_3(A')/3T_3(A')\ \subset\ T_3(A')/3T_3(A')=A'[3], \]

    both graded pieces are isomorphic to \(T_3(A')/\theta T_3(A')\simeq A'[\theta]\), via the \(G_{\Q}\)-linear map \(\theta\). Hence \(A'[3]^{\mathrm{ss}}\simeq(A'[\theta]^{\mathrm{ss}})^{\oplus2}\), and \(A'[\theta]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) has a trivial constituent.

  2. Passing to \(B[\theta]\). Since \(\zeta_3\equiv1\pmod{\theta}\), each \(\xi(g)\in\mu_3\) acts trivially on \(A'[\theta]\). By Section 8.3, \(B[\theta]\simeq A'[\theta]\) as \(G_{\Q}\)-modules, so \(B[\theta]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) has a trivial constituent.

  3. Two lines in \(B[\theta]\). The group \(B[\theta]\) has order \(\deg\theta=9\) and is killed by \(\theta^2=-3\), so it is a \(2\)-dimensional \(\F_3\)-vector space. Put \(\Delta=\ker(\theta|_{E_0})\), a \(G_K\)-stable line in \(B[\theta]\), on which \(G_K\) acts by a character \(\alpha\). Fix \(\tau\in G_{\Q}\setminus G_K\). Then \(\tau\Delta=\ker(\theta|_{\tau E_0})\), and \(G_K\) acts on it by \(\alpha^\tau(g)=\alpha(\tau^{-1}g\tau)\). On the other hand \(\ker(\theta|_{\tau E_0})=\theta(E_0[3])\): this image lies in \(\tau E_0=\theta E_0\), it is killed by \(\theta|_{\tau E_0}\) because \(\theta^2=-3\), and both groups have order \(3\). As \(G_K\)-modules, \(\theta(E_0[3])\simeq E_0[3]/\Delta\), and \(E_0[3]/\Delta\simeq\Hom(\Delta,\mu_3)\) by the Weil pairing. Hence

    \[ \alpha^\tau=\alpha^{-1}\,\bar\chi_3|_{G_K}. \]

  4. Case \(\tau\Delta=\Delta\). Then \(\alpha^\tau=\alpha\), so \(\bar\chi_3|_{G_K}=\alpha^2=1\), because \(\alpha\) takes values in \(\F_3^\times=\{\pm1\}\).

  5. Case \(\tau\Delta\ne\Delta\). Then \(B[\theta]=\Delta\oplus\tau\Delta\), with \(G_K\)-composition factors \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^\tau\). A trivial \(G_{\Q}\)-composition factor restricts to a trivial \(G_K\)-composition factor, so \(\alpha=1\) or \(\alpha^\tau=1\). Each of these implies the other, so \(\bar\chi_3|_{G_K}=\alpha\alpha^\tau=1\).

In both cases \(\zeta_3\in K\), that is, \(K=\Q(\sqrt{-3})\). This contradicts Section 8.1. ∎

11. Completion of the proof and remarks

Let \(P\in A(\Q)\) have order \(N\in\{26,39\}\). Type (Q) and type (N) are impossible by Section 3, and type (CM) by Proposition 4.2. If \(A\) has type (S), Proposition 7.1 gives \(\Gamma\simeq C_6\), and then Proposition 9.1, applied to \(P_{13}\), gives a contradiction. For \(N=39\), Lemma 10.1, applied to \(P_3\), gives a second contradiction, which does not use the computation on \(X_0(39)\). This proves the theorem. ∎

Remarks.

  • The point of order \(2\) or \(3\) is used only in type (N) and for \(\Gamma\in\{C_2,C_3,C_4\}\), through Lemma 2.1, and in type (CM), where the whole point of order \(N\) enters the point count of Proposition 4.2. The argument excludes rational \(13\)-torsion by itself only in type (Q), for \(\Gamma\in\{D_4,D_6,D_8,D_{12}\}\), and for \(\Gamma=C_6\).

  • Torsion is never moved along an arbitrary isogeny. Only residual semisimplifications are compared, and points are combined only inside one elliptic isogeny class over one field (Lemma 2.1).

  • The census curve 19.b, \(y^2+(x^3+x^2+1)y=x^2+x\), has Jacobian \(J_1(13)\) (LMFDB label 169.a.169.1). The LMFDB lists its Sato–Tate group as \(E_6\) and its endomorphism field as the real cyclic sextic field \(\Q(\zeta_{13})^+\), so it has type (S) with \(\Gamma\simeq C_6\), and \(J_1(13)(\Q)\simeq\Z/19\). The supplementary script checks that the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius at \(2\) has no root modulo \(13\) and that \(\#J_1(13)(\F_2)=19\). Hence \(J_1(13)[13]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) has no one-dimensional constituent and \(J_1(13)[3]^{\mathrm{ss}}\) has no trivial constituent, as Proposition 9.1 and Lemma 10.1 require.

  • The fibres of \(X_0(39)\to X_0(39)/w_3\) above the two non-cuspidal rational points consist of non-CM points over the imaginary field \(\Q(\sqrt{-7})\) that \(w_3\) maps to their conjugates. Thus the reality of \(K\) (Section 8.1) is essential in Proposition 9.1.

12. Consequence for the census

The groups [26], [2,26] and [39] contain an element of order \(26\) or \(39\). They are therefore impossible in the class “geometrically split, simple over \(\Q\)”. They are also impossible in the class “split over \(\Q\)” by the prime torsion obstruction, since \(13\) divides their orders. Hence their combined geometrically split ∞? cells are \(\emptyset\). The argument applies to every geometric splitting degree, to every polarization, and to every integral endomorphism ring.

The proof of the [13] obstruction, which treats rational \(13\)-torsion on its own, gives an independent argument for these three groups in the class “geometrically split, simple over \(\Q\)”. The present proof does not use it.
