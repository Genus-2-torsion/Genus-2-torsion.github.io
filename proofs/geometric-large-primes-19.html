Rational torsion primes of geometrically split genus-2 Jacobians

19 November 2026

Theorem 1. Let \(A/\Q\) be an abelian surface isogenous over \(\overline{\Q}\) to a product of elliptic curves. Every prime divisor of \(\#A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) is at most \(19\). If \(19\) divides this order, then \(A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\simeq\Z/19\Z\).

Corollary 2. The primes occurring as orders of rational points on geometrically split genus-2 Jacobians over \(\Q\) are exactly \(2,3,5,7,11,19\).

Elliptic torsion over quartic and sextic fields

Let \(S(d)\) denote the set of primes occurring as point orders on elliptic curves over number fields of degree \(d\). The primes in \(S(4)\) are precisely those at most \(17\). The primes in \(S(6)\) are those at most \(19\), together with \(37\). Every elliptic curve with a point of order \(37\) over a sextic field has \(j=-9317\). Derickx–Kamienny–Stein–Stoll, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 1.4.

The only elliptic torsion group over a sextic field whose order is divisible by \(19\) is \(\Z/19\Z\). We use this classification in the second part of Theorem 1. Najman, Theorem 1.1.

Reduction to a real field of degree three or six

Lemma 3. Suppose \(A\) is as in Theorem 1 and \(P\in A(\Q)\) has prime order \(p\ge19\). Then \(A\) has everywhere potentially good reduction, or there is a totally real cyclic extension \(L/\Q\) of degree \(3\) or \(6\) over which \(A_L\) is isogenous to the square of an elliptic curve \(E/L\).

Proof. Suppose first that the two geometric elliptic factors are nonisogenous. Their images in \(A_{\overline{\Q}}\) are its only elliptic subvarieties. Indeed, an elliptic subvariety is isogenous to one of the two factors, and its map to the quotient by the image of that factor is zero. Galois therefore permutes the two images, which are both defined over an extension of degree at most two. The point \(P\) either lies on one of these elliptic subvarieties or has order \(p\) in its elliptic quotient. Extending the field to degree four gives \(p\in S(4)\), a contradiction.

If \(A\) is geometrically the square up to isogeny of an elliptic curve with CM, it has everywhere potentially good reduction. For a square without CM, put \(D=\End^0(A_{\overline{\Q}})=M_2(\Q)\), let \(L\) be the field of definition of all geometric endomorphisms, and write \(\Gamma=\Gal(L/\Q)\). Its action on \(D\) embeds it in \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\Q)\). It is cyclic of order \(1,2,3,4,6\), or dihedral of order \(4,6,8,12\). Over \(L\) the diagonal matrix units cut out elliptic subvarieties up to isogeny, and the off-diagonal units give isogenies between them. Thus \(A_L\sim_L E^2\). In degrees \(1,2,4\) the same argument with an elliptic subvariety and its quotient contradicts \(S(4)\). Classification of the endomorphism field, §2.

Suppose \(\Gamma\) is dihedral of order \(2m\), where \(m=3,4,6\). Normalize a matrix \(R\) representing a generating rotation to have trace one. Its eigenvalue ratio is a primitive \(m\)-th root \(\xi\), so \(1/\det R=2+\xi+\xi^{-1}=1,2,3\), respectively. The characteristic polynomial of \(R\) has discriminant \(-3,-1,-1/3\), respectively, and \(B=\Z_p[R]\) is quadratic étale for \(p\ge19\). Reflections carry \(R\) to the trace-one representative of its projective inverse, \(1-R\). Thus \(\Gamma\) normalizes \(B\).

If \(B\) is unramified quadratic, \(R\) is a unit with nonscalar residue, so the unramified-order lemma applies. If \(B\) splits, the reflections exchange its idempotents. The rotation acts on the off-diagonal line by \(\xi\), whose reduction still has order \(m\) since \(p\nmid m\). The split-block lemma applies. In each case a rational \(p\)-point forces \(\Q(\zeta_p)\subseteq L\). The dihedral group would then have a cyclic quotient of order \(p-1\), whereas its abelianization has exponent at most two. Lemmas 6 and 7 of the order-13 obstruction.

The remaining possibilities for \(\Gamma\) are cyclic of order \(3\) or \(6\). Complex conjugation acts on \(M_2(\mathbb R)\) either trivially or by conjugation by a matrix of negative determinant. A generator of a finite cyclic subgroup of \(\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb R)\) of order greater than two has nonreal conjugate eigenvalues, hence positive determinant. Every element of \(\Gamma\) therefore has positive determinant sign and complex conjugation acts trivially on the endomorphism field. Thus \(L\) is totally real. ∎ Complex-conjugation lemma.

Proof of the prime bound

If \(A\) has everywhere potentially good reduction, Clark’s list of possible rational torsion orders contains no order divisible by a prime greater than \(19\). Its only multiple of \(19\) is \(19\) itself. Clark, Theorem 5, Clark–Xarles, Theorem 1.4.

In the other case of Lemma 3 take a sextic field \(H\) containing \(L\), using \(H=L\) when \([L:\Q]=6\). An elliptic subvariety of \(A_L\) or its elliptic quotient has an \(L\)-point of order \(p\), hence \(p\in S(6)\). The only candidate above \(19\) is \(37\). For this prime the elliptic curve carrying the point has integral \(j=-9317\), so has potentially good reduction at every finite place. Since \(A\) is geometrically isogenous to its square, the same holds for \(A\). Clark’s theorem now excludes \(37\).

Proof that a rational point of order 19 forces exact [19]

Suppose \(19\mid\#A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\). Clark’s list settles the potentially good reduction case. In the remaining case use \(L,E,H\) as above. For each prime \(q\) dividing the rational torsion order, project a rational \(q\)-point to an elliptic quotient, or retain it in the elliptic kernel if its image is zero. Both elliptic curves belong to the \(L\)-isogeny class of \(E\).

If \(q\ne19\), choose \(B,C\) in this class carrying \(L\)-points of orders \(19,q\), respectively. Factor an \(L\)-isogeny \(f:B\to C\) through \(B'=B/(\ker f)[q^\infty]\). The first isogeny preserves the order of the \(19\)-point. The second has degree prime to \(q\), so identifies \(B'[q]\) with \(C[q]\) over \(L\). Hence \(B'(L)\), and thus \(B'(H)\), has a point of order \(19q\). The sextic torsion classification excludes this.

To bound the exponent, put \(V=V_{19}(E)\). In \(V_{19}(A)=V\otimes W\) the multiplicity space \(W=\Q_{19}^2\) has trivial \(G_L\)-action. Take complex conjugation \(c\in G_L\) at a real embedding of \(L\). It has determinant \(-1\) on \(V\). Choose eigenvectors \(e_+,e_-\) of eigenvalues \(+1,-1\). The projectors \((1\pm c)/2\) preserve \(T=T_{19}(A)\), giving \[ T=(e_+\otimes\Lambda_+)\oplus(e_-\otimes\Lambda_-) \] for two full lattices in \(W\). By elementary divisors there is a basis \(w_1,w_2\) of \(W\) in which both lattices are diagonal. Each space \(V_i=V\otimes\Q_{19} w_i\) is \(G_L\)-stable, and \(T=(T\cap V_1)\oplus(T\cap V_2)\).

Identify \(V_i\) with \(V\). After scaling, \(T\cap V_i\) contains \(T_{19}(E)\), and its quotient by \(T_{19}(E)\) is a finite \(G_L\)-stable subgroup of \(E[19^\infty]\). Quotienting \(E\) by this subgroup realizes the lattice as the Tate module of an elliptic curve \(E_i/L\). Taking \(G_L\)-invariants in \(V_{19}(A)/T\) gives \(A(L)[19^\infty]\simeq E_1(L)[19^\infty]\oplus E_2(L)[19^\infty]\). Both summands have exponent at most \(19\) by the sextic classification over \(H\). Related odd-prime lattice argument, Theorem 5(a).

Finally \(A\) is simple over \(\Q\). Otherwise a rational elliptic subvariety or its quotient would have a rational \(19\)-point, which Mazur’s theorem excludes. The rational \(p\)-rank bound for geometrically split surfaces simple over \(\Q\) now gives \(\dim_{\F_{19}}A(\Q)[19]\le1\). The exponent bound and the exclusion of all other primes give \(A(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\simeq\Z/19\Z\). ∎ Rational \(p\)-rank theorem.

Proof of the exact prime list

Of the primes at most \(19\), the order-13 theorem for genus-2 Jacobians and the order-17 theorem for abelian surfaces exclude \(13\) and \(17\). For each remaining prime the census supplies a geometrically split genus-2 Jacobian whose full rational torsion group is cyclic of that prime order. The examples for \(2,3,5,7,11,19\) are, respectively, 2.c, 3.b, 5.c, 7.c, 11.b and 19.b. ∎

In particular, a genus-2 Jacobian over \(\Q\) with a rational point of order \(37,38,41,43,57,95\) or \(361\) is geometrically simple. More generally, this follows whenever its rational torsion has a prime divisor at least \(23\), or properly contains a subgroup of order \(19\).
