Rational \(7\)-torsion forces \(2\)-primary torsion of order at most \(2\) on principally polarized abelian surfaces that are geometrically split and simple over \(\Q\)

14 November 2026 · Geometrically split, simple over ℚ

Theorem. Let \(A/\Q\) be an abelian surface with a principal polarization defined over \(\Q\), and suppose that \(A\) is simple over \(\Q\) and geometrically split. If \(A(\Q)\) has a point of order \(7\), then

\[ \#A(\Q)[2^\infty]\le2 . \]

Corollary. No genus-\(2\) Jacobian over \(\Q\) that is geometrically split and simple over \(\Q\) has a rational point of order \(7\) and at least four rational points of \(2\)-power order. In particular [28] and [2,14] do not occur as the rational torsion subgroup of such a Jacobian. Neither do \([2,28]\) and \([2,2,14]\), which the census proofs [P228] and [P2214] had already excluded.

Here “geometrically split” means that \(A_{\bar\Q}\) is isogenous to a product of two elliptic curves; we impose no restriction on the degree of this isogeny or on the field of definition of the geometric endomorphisms. The Jacobian of a curve of genus \(2\) over \(\Q\) carries its canonical principal polarization, which is defined over \(\Q\). The bound is sharp: the census curve 14.c (LMFDB 9604.b3) is geometrically split and simple over \(\Q\), with rational torsion subgroup \([14]\). The theorem sharpens the bound \(\#A(\Q)[2^\infty]\le4\) of [P228]. The principal polarization is needed: Remark 1 gives an abelian surface over \(\Q\), simple over \(\Q\) and geometrically split, with rational torsion subgroup exactly \(\Z/2\times\Z/14\), whose polarizations over \(\Q\) all have type \((k,2k)\).

Outline. We follow the case distinction of [P2214] and [P228]. In the CM case we reduce at a prime \(p\ge5\) with \(p\not\equiv\pm1\pmod7\) that is inert in the CM field; this gives a supersingular abelian surface over \(\F_p\), and for such \(p\) no supersingular abelian surface over \(\F_p\) has a number of points divisible by \(28\) (Section 2). Cyclic endomorphism fields of degree \(3\) and \(6\) were settled in [P228]. In the remaining (quadratic) case, a curve in the \(K\)-isogeny class of an elliptic curve \(E\subset A_K\) has a \(K\)-point of order \(14\), so it comes from a quadratic point of the elliptic modular curve \(X_1(14)\). These lie in finitely many one-parameter families, and up to diamond operators in two of them, \(\mathcal A\) and \(\mathcal B\) (Section 5). When the complement of \(E\) for the principal polarization is \(E^\sigma\), the surface is a conjugate gluing of \(E\) with \(E^\sigma\); then the descent of the gluing and the existence of a fourth rational \(2\)-power point force two norm conditions on the \(2\)-division data of \(E\) (Lemma 8). In family \(\mathcal B\) the first condition already has no solutions; in family \(\mathcal A\) the two conditions together have none (Section 9, Chabauty and elliptic Chabauty). When \(A_{\bar\Q}\) is a square, the curve is a \(\Q\)-curve; on family \(\mathcal A\) it reduces to a cusp modulo every prime above \(3\), and the valuations of the \(j\)-invariants there show that its \(\Q\)-curve degree is a square. This forces \(\End^0_{\Q}(A)\cong\Q(i)\), and then again \(E^\sigma\) is the complement of \(E\) (Section 8).

1. Conventions and inputs

All number fields lie in a fixed \(\bar\Q\); \(G_k=\Gal(\bar\Q/k)\). For a quadratic field \(K=\Q(\sqrt d)\), \(\sigma\) is its nontrivial automorphism and \(\tilde\sigma\in G_{\Q}\) a lift; for a variety \(Y\) over \(K\), \(Y^\sigma\) is the conjugate variety and \(\tilde\sigma:Y(\bar\Q)\to Y^\sigma(\bar\Q)\) the induced bijection [P2214, Section 1]. \(\Q^{\times2}\), \(K^{\times2}\) are the nonzero squares, and \(e_2\) is the Weil pairing on \(2\)-torsion. A polarization \(\lambda:A\to A^\vee\) is defined over \(\Q\) if the isogeny \(\lambda\) is.

  • (I1) Quadratic torsion [KM, Theorem (0.1)], in the form of [P2214, (I1) and Lemma 1]: no elliptic curve over a quadratic field \(K\) has a subgroup \(\Z/28\) or \(\Z/2\times\Z/14\); and if a \(K\)-isogeny class contains a curve with a \(K\)-point of order \(7\), every curve \(E''\) in it has \(\#E''(K)[2^\infty]\le2\).

  • (I2) Reduction [ST], as in [P2214, (I2)]: good reduction is detected by inertia and preserved by isogenies; prime-to-\(p\) torsion injects into the reduction; potentially good reduction becomes good over a finite extension with the same residue field.

  • (I3) Case distinction [P2214, (I3)]: a geometrically split abelian surface \(A/\Q\) is of exactly one of the types (N) (\(A_{\bar\Q}\) is isogenous to \(E_1\times E_2\) with \(E_1\), \(E_2\) not isogenous), (CM) (\(A_{\bar\Q}\) is isogenous to \(E^2\) with \(E\) a CM curve) and (S) (\(A_{\bar\Q}\) is isogenous to \(E^2\) with \(E\) without CM). In type (S), \(L\) is the smallest field over which all geometric endomorphisms are defined and \(\Gamma=\Gal(L/\Q)\). (I4) [P63, Lemma 2]: a point of prime order \(q\) of an abelian surface \(X\) over \(k\) lies on a given elliptic curve \(C\subset X\) over \(k\) or maps to a point of order \(q\) of \(X/C\). (I5) [P63, Lemma 3]: in type (S) every elliptic curve in \(A_{\bar\Q}\) is defined over \(L\), and any two are \(L\)-isogenous.

  • (I6) [P2214, (I6)] ([P63, Proposition 8]): in type (S), simple over \(\Q\), with a rational point of order \(7\), \(\Gamma\) is cyclic of order \(2\), \(3\) or \(6\). (I7) [P228, Sections 2–5]: if moreover \(A\) has a principal polarization defined over \(\Q\) and \(\Gamma\) is cyclic of order \(3\) or \(6\), then \(A(\Q)[2]=0\). Also \(\#A(\Q)[2^\infty]\le4\) in all types [P228, Theorem].

  • (I8) Weil restriction [P2210, (I7) and Lemma 5]: for an elliptic curve \(E\) over \(K\), \((\Res_{K/\Q}E)(\bar\Q)=E(\bar\Q)\times E^\sigma(\bar\Q)\), where \(g\in G_K\) acts by \((x,y)\mapsto(gx,gy)\) and \(g\in G_{\Q}\setminus G_K\) by \((x,y)\mapsto(gy,gx)\), and \((\Res_{K/\Q}E)(\Q)=E(K)\). If \(E\subset A_K\) is an elliptic curve over \(K\) with \(E^\sigma\neq E\) and \(E'\) is \(K\)-isogenous to \(E\), then \(A\) is \(\Q\)-isogenous to \(\Res_{K/\Q}E'\), and \(\#\widetilde A(\F_p)=\#\widetilde{E'}(\F_{p^2})\) at every odd prime \(p\) inert in \(K\) of good reduction for \(A\). The map \((x,y)\mapsto x+y\) is an isogeny \(\Res_{K/\Q}E\to A\) over \(\Q\) with kernel \(\{(x,-x):x\in E\cap E^\sigma\}\).

  • (I9) Complements [BL, Section 5.3], [P45, proof of Lemma 3]. Let \(\lambda\) be a principal polarization of an abelian surface \(X\) over \(k\), \(E\subset X\) an elliptic curve, \(i_E\) the inclusion and \(i_E^\vee\lambda i_E=n\lambda_E\), with \(\lambda_E\) the canonical principal polarization of \(E\). The complement \(E^\perp\) (the connected component of \(\ker(i_E^\vee\lambda)\)) is an elliptic curve; \(E\cap E^\perp=E[n]= E^\perp[n]\); \(\pi_E=\lambda_E^{-1}i_E^\vee\lambda:X\to E\) has kernel \(E^\perp\) and \(\pi_Ei_E=n\); the addition map \(E\times E^\perp\to X\) has kernel \(\{(x,-x):x\in E[n]\}\); the norm endomorphism \(N_E=i_E\pi_E\) is symmetric for the Rosati involution [BL, Lemma 5.3.1], with image \(E\) and kernel \(\ker(i_E^\vee\lambda)\). If \(X\) and \(\lambda\) are defined over \(\Q\), then \(g(E^\perp)=(gE)^\perp\) and \(g(N_E)=N_{gE}\) for \(g\in G_{\Q}\), since these constructions commute with conjugation: \(g(i_E)=i_{gE}\), \(g(\lambda_E)=\lambda_{gE}\) and \(g(\lambda)=\lambda\).

  • (I10) Kubert's normal form for \(\Z/7\) [Ku, Table 3]: every pair \((E,P)\) of an elliptic curve over a field \(k\) of characteristic \(0\) and \(P\in E(k)\) of order \(7\) is isomorphic to \((E_7(t),(0,0))\), \(E_7(t):y^2+(1-c)xy-by\)\({}=x^3-bx^2\), \(b=t^3-t^2\), \(c=t^2-t\), for some \(t\in k\) with \(E_7(t)\) nonsingular.

  • (I11) The \(j\)-function at a prime [Si, Theorem V.3.1(b) and Lemma V.5.1]. Let \(j=q^{-1}+\sum_{n\ge0}c(n)q^n\), \(c(n)\in\Z\), be the \(q\)-expansion of the elliptic modular function. Let \(L\) be a finite extension of \(\Q_p\) and \(v\) the valuation of \(\bar L\) with \(v(p)=1\). For \(q\) in a finite extension of \(L\) with \(v(q)>0\), the series \(j(q)=q^{-1}+\sum c(n)q^n\) converges, and \(v(j(q))=-v(q)\). Every \(\alpha\in\bar L\) with \(v(\alpha)<0\) equals \(j(q)\) for a unique \(q\in\Q_p(\alpha)\) with \(v(q)>0\).

  • (I12) Deuring's criterion [La, Chapter 13, Theorem 12], due to Deuring [De]: an elliptic curve over a number field with complex multiplication by an order in an imaginary quadratic field \(M\), with good reduction at a prime above a prime \(p\) that is inert in \(M\), has supersingular reduction there.

  • (I13) The modular polynomial [La, Chapter 5, Sections 1–3]. For \(N\ge1\) the modular polynomial \(\Phi_N\in\Z[X,Y]\) satisfies, in \(\Q(\zeta_N)((q^{1/N}))[X]\),

    \[ \Phi_N(X,j(q))=\prod_{a,b,d}\bigl(X-j(\zeta_d^b\,q^{a/d})\bigr), \]

    the product over the integers \(a,d>0\) with \(ad=N\) and \(0\le b<d\) with \(\gcd(a,b,d)=1\), where \(\zeta_d=\zeta_N^{N/d}\) and \(j(\zeta_d^bq^{a/d})\) is the series of (I11) evaluated at \(\zeta_d^bq^{a/d}\) (formula (1) of [La, Chapter 5, Section 2]). If \(B\to A\) is a cyclic isogeny of degree \(N\) of elliptic curves over a field of characteristic \(0\), then \(\Phi_N(j(B),j(A))=0\) [La, Chapter 5, Theorem 5 and the remark following it].

2. The CM case

Lemma 1. Let \(p\ge5\) be prime and \(B\) an abelian surface over \(\F_p\) whose Frobenius eigenvalues are \(\sqrt p\) times roots of unity. Then \(\#B(\F_p)\)\({}\in\{(p+1)^2-kp:0\le k\)\({}\le4\}\), or \(p=5\) and \(\#B(\F_5)\in\{11,71\}\). In particular, if \(28\mid\#B(\F_p)\), then \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod7\).

Proof. Write the eigenvalues as \(\alpha_i,p/\alpha_i\) (\(i=1,2\)) and \(\beta_i=\alpha_i+p/\alpha_i=\sqrt p\,\gamma_i\), where \(\gamma_i=\zeta_i+\zeta_i^{-1}\) for roots of unity \(\zeta_i\) of order \(n_i\). Then \(h(y)=(y-\beta_1)(y-\beta_2)\)\({}=y^2+a_1y+c\in\Z[y]\) and \(\#B(\F_p)=h(p+1)\). Since \(c=p\gamma_1\gamma_2\) and \(\gamma_1\gamma_2\) is an algebraic integer, \(p\mid c\).

  • If \(\beta_1\in\Z\), then \(\beta_2\in\Z\) and \(\gamma_1^2=\beta_1^2/p\in\Q\); so \(\zeta_1^2+\zeta_1^{-2}\in\Q\) is one of \(0,\pm1,\pm2\) and \(\gamma_1^2\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}\). As \(p\ge5\), \(p\gamma_1^2\) is a perfect square only for \(\gamma_1=0\). So \(\beta_1=\beta_2=0\) and \(h=y^2\).

  • If \(\beta_1\notin\Z\) and \(\beta_1+\beta_2=0\), then \(\gamma_1^2=-c/p\in\Q\setminus\{0\}\), so \(\gamma_1^2\in\{1,2,3,4\}\) and \(h=y^2-kp\) with \(1\le k\le4\).

  • If \(\beta_1+\beta_2\ne0\), let \(\rho\) be an automorphism of the abelian field \(\Q(\sqrt p,\zeta_{n_1})\) with \(\rho(\beta_1)=\beta_2\) (such a \(\rho\) exists: \(\beta_1,\beta_2\) are conjugate). Then \(\rho(\sqrt p)=\pm\sqrt p\) and \(\rho(\gamma_1)=\gamma_1'\) is a conjugate of \(\gamma_1\), so \(-a_1=\beta_1+\beta_2\)\({}=\sqrt p(\gamma_1\pm\gamma_1')\)\({}\in\Z\setminus\{0\}\) and \(\sqrt p\in\Q(\zeta_{n_1})\) (the conjugate \(\gamma_1'\) lies in the Galois field \(\Q(\zeta_{n_1})\)). Moreover \(\gamma_1=\beta_1/\sqrt p\) lies in a field of degree at most \(4\), so \(\varphi(n_1)=2[\Q(\gamma_1):\Q]\le8\) (or \(\gamma_1\in\Q\)). As \(p\) ramifies in \(\Q(\sqrt p)\subseteq\Q(\zeta_{n_1})\), \(p\mid n_1\), hence \(p-1\le\varphi(n_1)\le8\) and \(p\in\{5,7\}\); and \(\Q(\sqrt7)\), of conductor \(28\), lies only in cyclotomic fields of degree at least \(\varphi(28)=12\). So \(p=5\). Then \(a_1/\sqrt5\) is an algebraic integer, so \(5\mid a_1\); with \(|a_1|=|\beta_1+\beta_2|\le4\sqrt5\) this gives \(a_1=\pm5\). Real roots in \([-2\sqrt5,2\sqrt5]\) require \(25-4c\ge0\) and \(20+c\ge10\sqrt5\), and \(5\mid c\), so \(c=5\) and \(h=y^2\pm5y+5\).

Hence \(\#B(\F_p)=(p+1)^2-kp\) with \(0\le k\le4\), or \(p=5\) and \(\#B(\F_5)=36\pm30+5\)\({}\in\{11,71\}\). As \(p\) is odd, \(4\mid(p+1)^2-kp\) forces \(4\mid kp\), so \(k\in\{0,4\}\) and \(\#B(\F_p)=(p\pm1)^2\); then \(7\mid\#B(\F_p)\) gives \(p\equiv\mp1\pmod7\). Neither \(11\) nor \(71\) is divisible by \(7\). ∎

Part A of the certificate lists, for \(p\in\{5,7,11,13,17,19,23\}\), all integer pairs \((a_1,a_2)\) with \(p\mid a_1\), \(p\mid a_2\) and real roots of \(y^2+a_1y+a_2-2p\) in \([-2\sqrt p,2\sqrt p]\), and checks that they are among the polynomials of the lemma, that the values \(P(1)\) are those of the lemma (with \(11\) and \(71\) for \(p=5\)), and that \(28\nmid P(1)\) when \(p\not\equiv\pm1\pmod7\).

Proposition 2. If \(A\) is of type (CM) and \(A(\Q)\) has a point of order \(7\), then \(\#A(\Q)[2^\infty]\le2\).

Proof. By [P2214, Section 3], \(\#A(\Q)[2^\infty]\le4\); suppose it is \(4\). Let \(M\) be the CM field of the elliptic curve \(E\) with \(A_{\bar\Q}\) isogenous to \(E^2\). Choose a prime \(p\ge5\), \(p\ne7\), inert in \(M\), with \(p\not\equiv\pm1\pmod 7\): if \(M\ne\Q(\sqrt{-7})\), then \(M\) and \(\Q(\zeta_7)\) are linearly disjoint (the only quadratic subfield of \(\Q(\zeta_7)\) is \(\Q(\sqrt{-7})\)), and Chebotarev's theorem gives infinitely many primes inert in \(M\) and \(\equiv2\pmod7\); if \(M=\Q(\sqrt{-7})\), take \(p=5\), which is inert since \(\bigl(\frac{-7}5\bigr)=-1\). As in [P2214, Section 3], \(A\) has good reduction \(\widetilde A\) over a finite extension of \(\Q_p\) with residue field \(\F_p\), and the subgroup of \(A(\Q)\) generated by the point of order \(7\) and \(A(\Q)[2^\infty]\), of order \(28\), injects into \(\widetilde A(\F_p)\) by (I2). Over a further finite extension, \(A\) is isogenous to \(E^2\) and \(E\) has good reduction \(\widetilde E\), which is supersingular by (I12); reductions of isogenous abelian varieties with good reduction are isogenous, so \(\widetilde A\) is isogenous over \(\bar\F_p\) to \(\widetilde E^2\). Over a finite field \(\F_{p^{2r}}\) over which this isogeny and all endomorphisms of \(\widetilde E\) are defined, the Frobenius of \(\widetilde E\) is \(\pm p^r\), so every Frobenius eigenvalue \(\alpha\) of \(\widetilde A\) over \(\F_p\) satisfies \(\alpha^{2r}=\pm p^r\). Lemma 1 with \(28\mid\#\widetilde A(\F_p)\) gives \(p\equiv\pm1\pmod7\), a contradiction. ∎

3. Endomorphism fields of degree 3 and 6

If \(A\) is of type (S), simple over \(\Q\), with a rational point of order \(7\), then \(\Gamma\) is cyclic of order \(2\), \(3\) or \(6\) by (I6). For order \(3\) or \(6\), and \(A\) with a principal polarization defined over \(\Q\), \(A(\Q)[2]=0\) by (I7), so \(A(\Q)[2^\infty]=0\). It remains to treat type (N) and type (S) with \(\Gamma\cong C_2\).

4. The quadratic case: a quadratic point of \(X_1(14)\)

Setting (Q). \(A/\Q\) is simple over \(\Q\) and either of type (N), with \(K\) the quadratic field over which the two elliptic curves of \(A_{\bar\Q}\) are defined, or of type (S) with \(\Gamma\cong C_2\) and \(K=L\). Every elliptic curve \(E\subset A_{\bar\Q}\) is defined over \(K\) and \(E^\sigma\neq E\) [P2214, Section 2].

Lemma 3. In setting (Q), suppose that \(A(\Q)\) has a point \(P_7\) of order \(7\) and \(\#A(\Q)[2^\infty]=4\). Let \(E\subset A_K\) be an elliptic curve. Then \(E(K)[2^\infty]=\langle T\rangle\cong\Z/2\); \(E\) has no \(K\)-rational cyclic subgroup of order \(4\); and there is a \(K\)-isogeny \(E\to E'\) of degree a power of \(7\) with \(E'(K)\) containing a point of order \(14\).

Proof. Put \(Y=A_K/E^\sigma\). By [P2214, Proposition 2, step 1], \(\#A(\Q)[2^\infty]\)\({}\le\#E(K)[2^\infty]\cdot\#Y(K)[2^\infty]\), and by (I4) \(E(K)\) or \(Y(K)\) has a point of order \(7\). By (I1) both factors are at most \(2\), so both equal \(2\). If \(C_4\subset E\) were a \(K\)-rational cyclic subgroup of order \(4\), then \(2C_4=\langle T\rangle\) and \(E_1=E/\langle T\rangle\) would have the two \(K\)-rational subgroups \(E[2]/\langle T\rangle\) and \(C_4/\langle T\rangle\) of order \(2\), hence \(E_1[2]\subseteq E_1(K)\), against (I1). The isogeny \(E\to E'\) is that of [P40, Lemma 9, Step 3] (the quotient by the \(7\)-primary part of the kernel of \(E\to Y\), or the identity); it maps \(T\) to a point of order \(2\) and \(E'(K)\) receives a point of order \(7\). ∎

5. The modular curve \(X_1(14)\)

Let \(X\) be the elliptic curve \(Y^2+XY+Y=X^3-X\) (Cremona \(14a4\)). On \(X\) put

\[ t=1-\frac{X(X-1)^2}{Y^2},\qquad s=-\frac{(X-1)^2}{2Y},\qquad e=\frac{b}{1-c-2ts},\qquad b=t^3-t^2,\ c=t^2-t . \]

Lemma 4.

  1. \(X(\Q)\)\({}=\{O,(0,0),(0,-1),(1,\)\({}0),(1,-2),(-1,0)\}\)\({}\cong\Z/6\), and \(X\) has rank \(0\); \((0,0)\) has order \(3\) and \((-1,0)\) order \(2\).

  2. For every field \(k\supseteq\Q\) and elliptic curve \(E/k\) with a point \(P\) of order \(14\) there is a point \(x\in X(k)\setminus X(\Q)\) such that \((E,8P)\cong(E_7(t(x)),(0,0))\) and \(7P\) corresponds to the \(2\)-torsion point of \(E_7(t(x))\) with \(x\)-coordinate \(e(x)\). The points of \(X(\Q)\) are the poles of \(j(E_7(t))\) on \(X\) (the rational cusps), with pole orders \(7\) at \(O,(0,0),(0,-1)\) and \(14\) at \((1,0),(1,-2),(-1,0)\).

  3. If \(x\in X(k)\) and \(j(E_7(t(x)))\notin\{0,1728,\infty\}\), then \(E_7(t(x+(0,0)))\cong E_7(t(x))\) over \(k\) (translation by \((0,0)\) is a diamond operator); \(x\mapsto-x\) does not preserve \(j\).

  4. The zeros of \(j(E_7(t))\) on \(X\) are places of degree \(6\) and \(18\), and those of \(j(E_7(t))-1728\) places of degree \(12\) and \(24\). So \(j(E_7(t(x)))\notin\{0,1728\}\) at every point \(x\) of \(X\) of degree at most \(2\).

Proof. Part B of the certificate checks (a) (the \(2\)-Selmer group has order \(2=\#X(\Q)[2]\)); that \(e\) is a root of the \(2\)-division polynomial of \(E_7(t)\) identically on \(X\); that \(X\) and \(Y\) are rational functions of \((t,e)\), so that \(\Q(X)=\Q(t,e)\); that \(t\) has degree \(3\) on \(X\); that the plane curve \(G(t,e)=0\) given by the \(2\)-division polynomial is absolutely irreducible, and that its singular points satisfy \(b\,\Delta(E_7(t))=0\); the pole orders of \(j\); (c): the \(j\)-invariants agree in \(\Q(X)\) and the ratio \(c_4'c_6/(c_4c_6')\) is a square \(u^2\) in \(\Q(X)\); and the degrees in (d). At a point with \(j\notin\{0,1728,\infty\}\) the four values \(c_4,c_6,c_4',c_6'\) are finite and nonzero, so \(u\) is regular and nonzero there and the two curves are isomorphic over \(k\). Since \(t\) has degree \(3\), \(G\) has degree \(3\) in \(e\) and \(\Q(X)=\Q(t,e)\), the map \(x\mapsto(t(x),e(x))\) is birational onto \(G=0\), and \(X\) is the normalization of its projective closure. For (b), (I10) gives \(t_0\in k\) with \((E,8P)\cong(E_7(t_0),(0,0))\), and \(7P\) gives a root \(e_0\in k\) of the \(2\)-division polynomial. As \(b(t_0)\Delta(E_7(t_0))\ne0\), the point \((t_0,e_0)\) is a smooth point of \(G=0\), so it has a unique preimage \(x\) on \(X\), which is therefore \(k\)-rational; \(x\notin X(\Q)\) because \(j(E)\) is finite. ∎

Quadratic points. Let \(x\in X(K)\setminus X(\Q)\), \(K\) quadratic, and \(R=x+x^\sigma\in X(\Q)\). If \(R=O\), then \(x^\sigma=-x\), i.e. \(x=(m,y)\) with \(m\in\Q\) and \(q_{O,m}(y)=0\), where \(q_{O,m}(Y)\)\({}=Y^2+(m+1)Y-(m^3-m)\). If \(R\ne O\), the line through \(x\), \(x^\sigma\) and \(-R\) is defined over \(\Q\) and not vertical, of slope \(m\in\Q\), and \(X(x)\) is a root of a monic quadratic \(q_{R,m}\in\Q[X]\) (as in [P2210, Lemma 7(c)]). In both cases \(q_{R,m}\) is irreducible and \(K\) is its splitting field. Translation by the \(3\)-torsion point \(\tau=(0,0)\) replaces \(R\) by \(R+2\tau\) and, by Lemma 4(c) and (d), does not change the curve \(E_7(t(x))\); and \(2k\tau\) (\(k=0,1,2\)) runs through the \(3\)-torsion \(\{O,(0,0),(0,-1)\}\). Hence every elliptic curve over a quadratic field with a point of order \(14\) is \(E_7(t(x))\) for a point \(x\) of

\[ \mathcal A:\ x^\sigma=-x,\quad x=(m,y),\ m\in\Q,\qquad\text{or}\qquad \mathcal B:\ x+x^\sigma=(-1,0). \]

In setting (Q) the elliptic curves \(E\subset A_K\) have no CM, which gives \(j(E)\notin\{0,1728\}\) independently of Lemma 4(d): in type (S) by definition, and in type (N) because if \(E\) had CM by an order in an imaginary quadratic field \(M\), then \(E^\sigma\) would have CM by an order in \(M\) as well, and \(E^\sigma\) would be isogenous to \(E\) over \(\bar\Q\), contrary to type (N).

6. Two-division invariants

Let \(E/K\) have \(E(K)[2]=\langle T\rangle\cong\Z/2\). In a model \(y^2=x(x^2+ax+\beta)\) with \(T=(0,0)\) put \(\Delta_E=a^2-4\beta\) and \(\beta_E=\beta\). Their classes in \(K^\times/K^{\times2}\) do not depend on the model; \(K(E[2])=K(\sqrt{\Delta_E})\) is quadratic over \(K\), and \(K(E_1[2])=K(\sqrt{\beta_E})\) for \(E_1=E/\langle T\rangle\), whose \(2\)-division quadratic has discriminant \(16\beta\). A \(K\)-isogeny of odd degree \(E\to E''\) preserves both classes, since it identifies \(E[2^\infty]\) with \(E''[2^\infty]\) and induces an odd isogeny \(E_1\to E''_1\).

The certificate (part V) checks that \(E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\Z/14\); that \(\Norm(\Delta_E)\in\Q^{\times2}\), so that \(K(E[2])\) is biquadratic over \(\Q\); that \(j(E)\) is not integral, so that \(E\) has no CM; and that the two primes \(\mathfrak p\), \(\mathfrak p'\) of \(K\) above \(31\) are of good reduction with \(a_{\mathfrak p}(E)=4\) and \(a_{\mathfrak p'}(E)=-10\). Hence \(E\) is not geometrically isogenous to \(E^\sigma\): otherwise, as \(\End(E_{\bar\Q})=\Z\), \(E^\sigma\) would be \(K\)-isogenous to a quadratic twist of \(E\), and \(|a_{\mathfrak p'}(E)|=|a_{\mathfrak p}(E^\sigma)|\)\({}=|a_{\mathfrak p}(E)|\). Let \(B=\Res_{K/\Q}E/\langle(T,\sigma T)\rangle\). Writing \(E[2]=\{0,T,P_1,P_2\}\), \(E^\sigma[2]=\{0,\sigma T,Q_1,Q_2\}\) with \(Q_i=\tilde\sigma P_i\) for a lift \(\tilde\sigma\) of order \(2\) on \(K(E[2])\), the classes of \((T,0)\), \((P_1,Q_1)\), \((P_1,Q_2)\) are fixed by \(G_K\) and by \(\tilde\sigma\) (e.g. \(\tilde\sigma(P_1,Q_1)=(\tilde\sigma^2P_1,Q_1)\)\({}=(P_1,Q_1)\), and the nontrivial element \(\theta\) of \(\Gal(K(E[2])/K)\) moves \((P_1,Q_1)\) by \((T,\sigma T)\)), so \(B(\Q)[2]\supseteq(\Z/2)^2\); the odd part of \(B(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) is \(E(K)[7]\); and \(\#B(\Q)[2^\infty]\le4\) by [P2214, Proposition 2], which needs no polarization. So \(B(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\Z/2\times\Z/14\). As \(E\) and \(E^\sigma\) are not geometrically isogenous, \(\End^0_{\Q}(B)=\Q\): \(B\) is simple over \(\Q\), geometrically split, and the classes of polarizations over \(\Q\) form a rank-one lattice. The descent \(\lambda'\) of \(2\lambda_0\) along \(\Res_{K/\Q}E\to B\) has degree \(16/4=4\), so it is primitive, and every polarization of \(B\) over \(\Q\) is \(k\lambda'\), of degree \(4k^4\ne1\): \(B\) is not principally polarizable over \(\Q\).

  2. \(\Q\)-curves with a point of order \(14\). Among the \(14\) elliptic curves over quadratic fields with torsion subgroup \(\Z/14\) in the LMFDB [LMFDB], exactly two are \(\Q\)-curves: 2.0.7.1-196.2-a3 and 2.0.7.1-196.2-a4, over \(K=\Q(\sqrt{-7})\). Let \(E\) be the first, \(y^2+xy+(a+1)y\)\({}=x^3+(a-1)x^2+(6\)\({}-5a)x+2a-1\) with \(a^2-a+2=0\). The certificate (part V) checks that \(E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\Z/14\); that \(j(E)\) is not integral, so that \(E\) has no CM; that \(E/\langle T\rangle\cong E^\sigma\), which is the second curve, so that \(E\) is a \(\Q\)-curve of degree \(2\); that \(E\cong E_7(t(x))\) for a quadratic point \(x\) of family \(\mathcal B\), at the exceptional value \(m=-\frac12\) of Section 9, with \(\Norm(\Delta_E)\) and \(\Norm(\beta_E)\) of class \(14\); and that \(j(E)\) is a unit at the prime \(3O_K\). This is consistent with Proposition 11: the degree \(2\) is not a square, and the point is not in family \(\mathcal A\). The census curve 14.c (LMFDB 9604.b3, table g2c_curves_new), which shows that the theorem is sharp, has \(\#J(\F_p)=\#(\Res_{K/\Q}E)(\F_p)\) for its Jacobian \(J\) at every prime \(5\le p<120\), \(p\ne7\) (certificate, part V), as it would if \(J\) were \(\Q\)-isogenous to \(\Res_{K/\Q}E\); the four curves of the LMFDB isogeny class 9604.b have rational torsion \([14]\), \([2]\), \([14]\), \([2]\).

  3. Validation (not used in the proof). (i) In the LMFDB genus-\(2\) tables g2c_curves and g2c_curves_new, the curves that are simple over \(\Q\), geometrically split and have \(7\mid\#J(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) have rational torsion \([7]\), \([14]\) or \([21]\), so none has \(2\)-primary torsion of order greater than \(2\). (ii) For the \(44\) quadratic points of family \(\mathcal A\) with \(m=a/b\), \(|a|\le6\), \(1\le b\le6\), the valuations \(v_{\mathfrak p}(j(E))\) and \(v_{\mathfrak p}(j(E^\sigma))\) are equal negative multiples of \(7\) at every prime \(\mathfrak p\) of \(K\) above \(3\) (certificate, part V), in accordance with the proof of Proposition 11.

  4. Where the hypotheses are used. The principal polarization, and the fact that it is defined over \(\Q\), enter through (I7) (cyclic cubic case, which needs a \(G_{\Q}\)-equivariant perfect pairing), through the complement \(E^\perp=E^\sigma\) (Lemma 8, type (N), where \(\pi_E\) must be defined over \(K\)) and through the Rosati argument of Corollary 12; Remark 1 shows that it cannot be dropped. Simplicity over \(\Q\) gives \(E^\sigma\ne E\) and the field \(\End^0_{\Q}(A)\).
