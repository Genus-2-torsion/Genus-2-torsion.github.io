Necessary conditions for genus-\(2\) Jacobians split over \(\Q\) with torsion \([25]\), \([32]\), \([2,28]\), \([2,2,20]\) or \([2,2,2,12]\)

15 November 2026 · Split over ℚ

Theorem. Let \(C/\Q\) be a curve of genus \(2\) whose Jacobian \(J\) is split over \(\Q\). Let \(E\subset J\) be an elliptic curve over \(\Q\), let \(F=E^\perp\) be its complement for the canonical principal polarization, and let \(n\) be the gluing degree, so that \(J\cong(E\times F)/\Gamma_\psi\), where \(\Gamma_\psi\) is the graph of a Galois-equivariant anti-isometry \(\psi\colon E[n]\to F[n]\) and \(n\ge2\). Put \(T_E=E(\Q)_\tors\) and \(T_F=F(\Q)_\tors\), and let \(N_X\) denote the conductor of \(X\).

  1. If \(J(\Q)_\tors\cong\Z/2\times\Z/28\), then \(\{T_E,T_F\}\)\({}=\{\Z/7,\ \Z/2\times\Z/4\}\), \(n\) is prime to \(14\), and \(E\) and \(F\) have split multiplicative reduction at \(3\) and at \(5\). In particular \(v_3(N_J)=v_5(N_J)=2\), so \(225\mid N_J\).

  2. If \(J(\Q)_\tors\cong(\Z/2)^2\times\Z/20\), then \(\{T_E,T_F\}\)\({}=\{\Z/10,\ \Z/2\times\Z/4\}\) and \(n\) is odd and prime to \(5\).

  3. If \(J(\Q)_\tors\cong(\Z/2)^3\times\Z/12\), then \(\{T_E,T_F\}\)\({}=\{\Z/2\times\Z/6,\ \Z/2\times\Z/4\}\), \(n\) is prime to \(6\), and \(E\) and \(F\) have multiplicative reduction at \(2\), \(3\), \(5\), \(7\), \(11\) and \(13\). In particular \(v_p(N_J)=2\) for these six primes, so \(30030^2=901800900\) divides \(N_J\).

  4. If \(J(\Q)_\tors\cong\Z/32\), then \(E\) and \(F\) are not isogenous over \(\Q\), and either \(\{T_E,T_F\}=\{\Z/8,\ \Z/4\}\), \(v_2(n)=2\) and \(j(E)\neq j(F)\), or \(T_E\cong T_F\cong\Z/8\), \(16\mid n\) and \(E\) and \(F\) are not isogenous over \(\bar\Q\).

  5. If \(J(\Q)_\tors\cong\Z/25\), then \(T_E\cong T_F\cong\Z/5\), \(5\mid n\), and: (i) \(E\) and \(F\) are semistable; (ii) at \(5\), either both have good reduction and \(\{\#\widetilde E(\F_5),\#\widetilde F(\F_5)\}=\{5,10\}\), or both have split multiplicative reduction, and the second case holds if \(25\mid n\); (iii) \(66\) divides both \(N_E\) and \(N_F\), or \(210\) divides both; (iv) relying on the completeness of Cremona's tables below conductor \(500000\), \(N_E\ge8778\), \(N_F\ge8778\) and \(\max(N_E,N_F)\ge500000\), so that \(N_J=N_EN_F\ge4389000000\).

Here “split over \(\Q\)” means that \(J\) is isogenous over \(\Q\) to a product of two elliptic curves over \(\Q\); then \(J\) contains elliptic curves over \(\Q\), and (I10) below attaches to each of them its complement, the gluing degree and the anti-isometry. For the Jacobian of a curve of genus \(2\) the gluing degree of every elliptic curve is at least \(2\). The five groups of the Theorem are the groups of the census that have neither an example nor an exclusion in the class split over \(\Q\) (15 November 2026): [25], [32], [2,28], [2,2,20] and [2,2,2,12]. The Theorem excludes none of them: it lists conditions that an example must satisfy. Part (d) rests on a statement about all abelian surfaces, Theorem 5: an abelian surface over \(\Q\) that is isogenous over \(\Q\) to the square of an elliptic curve has no rational point of order \(32\); no polarization is needed there.

Hypotheses. The condition \(n\ge2\) is needed in (a) and (c): for the product polarization (\(n=1\)) both statements are false, as 26b1 \(\times\) 15a1 and 30a2 \(\times\) 15a1 show (Remark 5). Parts (b), (d) and (e) hold for every principally polarized abelian surface over \(\Q\) that is split over \(\Q\), with the polarization defined over \(\Q\): part (b) does not use \(n\ge2\), and in (d) and (e) the torsion forces \(4\mid n\) and \(5\mid n\). All parts use that the torsion group is exactly the given one; part (e)(iv) also uses that \(T_E\) and \(T_F\) are exactly \(\Z/5\) (Remark 4).

Outline. Section 2 recalls the gluing and determines \(T_E\), \(T_F\) and the gluing degree in the five cases (Proposition 3). In each case a prime \(\ell\mid n\) gives an isomorphism \(E[\ell]\cong F[\ell]\) of Galois modules. For \([2,28]\) and \([2,2,2,12]\) we compare \(E[\ell]\) and \(F[\ell]\) at small primes \(p\) (Sections 5 and 6): the torsion of \(E\) and \(F\) restricts the reduction types and the traces of Frobenius, and every configuration other than the stated one gives traces that differ by an integer prime to \(\ell\) or, when \(\ell=p\), different unramified constituents of \(E[p]\) and \(F[p]\). For \([32]\) (Section 7) the curves cannot be isogenous over \(\Q\) by a lattice argument in the \(2\)-adic Tate module (Section 3); two curves with torsion \(\Z/8\) glued in degree divisible by \(16\) cannot be quadratic twists of each other, by a congruence of traces modulo \(16\); and for \(\Z/8\) and \(\Z/4\) with the same \(j\)-invariant the intermediate quotient by the \(2\)-torsion graph is a Weil restriction, whose rational torsion has exponent \(8\). For \([25]\) (Section 8) the point of order \(25\) gives a relation between the Kummer classes of the rational points of order \(5\) of \(E\) and \(F\) (Lemma 15), hence a congruence modulo \(25\) between the traces of Frobenius of \(E\) and \(F\) at all primes, with a scalar that does not depend on the prime. Local arguments at \(5\) and at small primes then give (i)–(iii); for (iv) we add an obstruction at nonsplit multiplicative primes and a complete computation in Cremona's tables below conductor \(500000\).

1. Conventions and inputs

All fields have characteristic \(0\), and \(G_k=\Gal(\bar k/k)\). For an abelian variety \(X\) over \(k\) and a prime \(\ell\), \(T_\ell(X)\) is the Tate module, \(V_\ell(X)=T_\ell(X)\otimes\Q_\ell\) and \(X[\ell^\infty]=V_\ell(X)/T_\ell(X)\); \(\chi_\ell\colon G_k\to\Z_\ell^\times\) is the \(\ell\)-adic cyclotomic character and \(\bar\chi_\ell\) its reduction modulo \(\ell\). For an abelian group \(M\), \(M[m]\) is its \(m\)-torsion and \(M[p^\infty]\) its \(p\)-primary part. We fix embeddings \(\bar\Q\subset\bar\Q_p\), so that \(G_{\Q_p}\subset G_{\Q}\), and write \(I_p\subset G_{\Q_p}\) for the inertia group; a Frobenius lift is an element of \(G_{\Q_p}\) inducing \(x\mapsto x^p\) on the residue field. For an elliptic curve \(E\) over \(\Q_p\) with good reduction, \(\widetilde E\) is its reduction and \(a_p(E)=p+1-\#\widetilde E(\F_p)\); we put \(t_p(E)=a_p(E)\) if \(E\) has good reduction, and \(t_p(E)=p+1\) or \(t_p(E)=-(p+1)\) if \(E\) has split or nonsplit multiplicative reduction. \(v_p\) is the \(p\)-adic valuation, \(\Delta\) a discriminant and \(c_4\), \(c_6\) the usual invariants of a Weierstrass equation.

  • (I1) Mazur's theorem [Ma, Theorem (8)]. For an elliptic curve \(E\) over \(\Q\), \(E(\Q)_\tors\) is \(\Z/m\) with \(1\le m\le10\) or \(m=12\), or \(\Z/2\times\Z/2m\) with \(1\le m\le4\).

  • (I2) Olson [Ol]. An elliptic curve over \(\Q\) with complex multiplication has torsion subgroup \(0\), \(\Z/2\), \(\Z/3\), \(\Z/4\), \(\Z/6\) or \((\Z/2)^2\); in particular it has no rational point of order \(5\) or \(8\).

  • (I3) Torsion over quadratic fields. By Kenku–Momose [KM, Theorem (0.1)] and Kamienny [Ka] (see the statement in [Na, Section 1]), the torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve over a quadratic field is \(\Z/m\) with \(1\le m\le16\) or \(m=18\), or \(\Z/2\times\Z/2m\) with \(1\le m\le6\), or \(\Z/3\times\Z/3\), \(\Z/3\times\Z/6\) or \(\Z/4\times\Z/4\). Hence a torsion subgroup containing \(\Z/2\times\Z/8\) is \(\Z/2\times\Z/8\) (certificate, Part E).

  • (I4) Universal families [Ku, Table 3], also in [HLP, Table 3]. Put \(E(b,c)\colon y^2+(1-c)xy-by\)\({}=x^3-bx^2\). Every pair \((E,P)\) of an elliptic curve over a field \(k\) and a point \(P\in E(k)\) of order \(5\) is isomorphic to \((E_d,(0,0))\), where \(E_d=E(d,d)\) and \(d\in k^\times\) with \(d^2-11d-1\neq0\). Every elliptic curve over \(\Q\) whose group of rational points contains a subgroup isomorphic to \(\Z/2\times\Z/6\) is isomorphic to \(E(c+c^2,c)\) with \(c=(10-2s)/(s^2-9)\) for some \(s\in\Q\).

  • (I5) Halving [Kn, Theorem 4.2]. If \(E\colon y^2\)\({}=(x-e_1)(x-e_2)(x-e_3)\) with \(e_i\in k\), then a point \((x_0,y_0)\in E(k)\) lies in \(2E(k)\) if and only if \(x_0-e_1\), \(x_0-e_2\) and \(x_0-e_3\) are squares in \(k\).

  • (I6) Reduction of elliptic curves [Si1, VII.1–VII.7]. Let \(E\) be an elliptic curve over \(\Q_p\) with a minimal Weierstrass equation (an integral equation with \(v_p(\Delta)<12\) is minimal [Si1, Remark VII.1.1]). The reduction is good, multiplicative or additive according as \(v_p(\Delta)=0\), \(v_p(\Delta)>0=v_p(c_4)\) or \(v_p(\Delta),v_p(c_4)>0\), and multiplicative reduction is split if the tangent lines at the node of the reduced curve are defined over \(\F_p\) [Si1, Proposition VII.5.1]. There are subgroups \(E_1(\Q_p)\subseteq E_0(\Q_p)\subseteq E(\Q_p)\) with \(E_1(\Q_p)\) the kernel of reduction, isomorphic to the group of points of the formal group of \(E\) [Si1, Propositions VII.2.1 and VII.2.2], and \(E_0(\Q_p)/E_1(\Q_p)\) is \(\widetilde E(\F_p)\), \(\F_p^\times\), a group of order \(p+1\), or \(\F_p\) in the good, split, nonsplit and additive cases [Si1, Proposition VII.5.1], [Si2, IV.9.4, Step 2]. The group \(E(\Q_p)/E_0(\Q_p)\) has order \(1\) or \(2\) in the nonsplit case [Si2, IV.9.4, Step 2] and at most \(4\) in the additive case [Si1, Theorem VII.6.1]. The torsion of \(E_1(\Q_p)\) has \(p\)-power order [Si1, Propositions IV.3.2(b) and VII.3.1]. \(E\) has potentially good reduction if and only if \(v_p(j(E))\ge0\) [Si1, Proposition VII.5.5]; the reduction type does not change in an unramified extension [Si1, Proposition VII.5.4(a)].

  • (I7) The criterion of Néron, Ogg and Shafarevich [Si1, Theorem VII.7.1 and Corollary VII.7.2], [ST, §1, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2]. Let \(X\) be an abelian variety over a finite extension \(K\) of \(\Q_p\) and \(\ell\neq p\). Then \(X\) has good reduction if and only if the inertia group of \(K\) acts trivially on \(T_\ell(X)\), and isogenous abelian varieties over \(K\) have good reduction together.

  • (I8) Tate curves [Si2, Theorems V.3.1 and V.5.3, Lemma V.5.2, Corollary V.5.4 and Proposition V.6.1]. If \(E/\Q_p\) has split multiplicative reduction, then \(E\cong E_q\) for a \(q\in p\Z_p\) with \(v_p(q)=-v_p(j(E))\)\({}=v_p(\Delta_{\min})\), and \(E(\bar\Q_p)\cong\bar\Q_p^\times/q^{\Z}\) compatibly with \(G_{\Q_p}\). Hence for \(m\ge1\) there is an exact sequence of \(G_{\Q_p}\)-modules \(0\to\mu_m\to E[m]\to\Z/m\to0\) in which a point represented by an \(m\)-th root of \(q\) maps to a generator, and similarly \(0\to\Z_\ell(1)\to T_\ell(E)\to\Z_\ell\to0\). If \(E\) has nonsplit multiplicative reduction, it becomes split over the unramified quadratic extension of \(\Q_p\), so \(T_\ell(E)\cong\eta\otimes T_\ell(E_q)\) with \(\eta\) the unramified quadratic character. If \(\ell\ge3\) is a prime not dividing \(v_p(q)\), some element of \(I_p\) acts on \(E[\ell]\) by a transvection. More generally, if \(v_p(j(E))<0\), then \(E\) is the quadratic twist of the Tate curve \(E_q\) with \(j(E_q)=j(E)\) by a quadratic character \(\varepsilon\), so \(T_\ell(E)\cong\varepsilon\otimes T_\ell(E_q)\); \(\varepsilon\) is unramified if and only if \(E\) has multiplicative reduction, by [Si1, Proposition VII.5.4(a)].

  • (I9) Local mod-\(p\) representations [Se, §1.11, Propositions 11 and 12, §1.12, Proposition 13 and the Corollaries]. Let \(E\) be an elliptic curve with good ordinary reduction over a finite extension of \(\Q_p\), of any ramification index. Then the kernel of the reduction map \(E[p]\to \widetilde E[p](\bar\F_p)\cong\Z/p\) is cyclic of order \(p\), and the map is onto. Now let \(E/\Q_p\) with \(p\ge3\). If \(E\) has good ordinary reduction, \(I_p\) acts on this kernel by \(\bar\chi_p\) and trivially on the quotient \(\widetilde E[p](\bar\F_p)\). If \(E\) has good supersingular reduction, \(I_p\) acts on \(E[p]\) through a cyclic group of order \(p^2-1\), irreducibly. If \(E\) has multiplicative reduction, \(E[p]\) is as in (I8).

  • (I10) Complements and gluing [BL, Sections 5.3 and 12.1], [BD, Section 2]. Let \((A,\lambda)\) be a principally polarized abelian surface over \(k\), with \(\lambda\) defined over \(k\), and let \(E\subset A\) be an elliptic curve over \(k\), with inclusion \(i_E\) and canonical principal polarization \(\lambda_E\). There is an integer \(n\ge1\), the gluing degree, with \(i_E^\vee\lambda i_E=n\lambda_E\). The complement \(F=E^\perp\) (the connected component of \(\ker(i_E^\vee\lambda)\)) is an elliptic curve over \(k\) with \(i_F^\vee\lambda i_F=n\lambda_F\), and \(E\) is the complement of \(F\) [BL, Corollaries 12.1.2 and 12.1.5]. The homomorphism \(\pi_E=\lambda_E^{-1}i_E^\vee\lambda\colon A\to E\) has kernel \(F\), and \(\pi_F\colon A\to F\), defined in the same way, has kernel \(E\) [BL, Proposition 12.1.3]; so \(A/F\cong E\) and \(A/E\cong F\). Moreover \(\pi_Ei_E=n\), \(\pi_Ei_F=0\), and \(E\cap F=E[n]=F[n]\) inside \(A\) [BL, Corollary 12.1.4]. The addition map \(\sigma\colon E\times F\to A\) and \(\alpha=(\pi_E,\pi_F)\colon A\to E\times F\) satisfy \(\alpha\sigma=[n]\) and \(\sigma\alpha=[n]\) [BL, §12.1, equation (3)]; the kernel of \(\sigma\) is \(\{(x,-x):x\in E\cap F\}\), the graph \(\Gamma_\psi\) of \(\psi\colon E[n]\to F[n]\), \(x\mapsto-x\), and \(\sigma^*\lambda=n\lambda_E\times n\lambda_F\) [BL, Lemma 12.1.6]. Hence \(\Gamma_\psi\) is isotropic for the product of the Weil pairings, that is, \(\psi\) is an anti-isometry [BD, Lemma 2.4 and the proof of Lemma 2.6]. All these maps are defined over \(k\), since the constructions commute with Galois conjugation; in particular \(\psi\) is \(G_k\)-equivariant. If \(n=1\), then \(\sigma\) is an isomorphism of principally polarized surfaces \((E\times F,\lambda_E\times\lambda_F)\cong(A,\lambda)\). The Jacobian of a curve of genus \(2\) is not a polarized product of elliptic curves, since its theta divisor is the irreducible curve [BL, Corollary 11.8.2]; so \(n\ge2\) for every elliptic curve in such a Jacobian.

  • (I11) Reduction of abelian varieties. Let \(X\) be an abelian variety with good reduction over the ring of integers \(O\) of a finite extension \(K\) of \(\Q_p\), with residue field \(\kappa\). The kernel of reduction \(X(K)\to\widetilde X(\kappa)\) is the group \(\Phi(\mathfrak m)\) of points of a commutative formal group \(\Phi\) over \(O\) of dimension \(\dim X\), \(\mathfrak m\) the maximal ideal [HS, Theorem C.2.6], and \(\Phi(\mathfrak m)\) has no torsion of order prime to \(p\) [HS, Proposition C.2.5]; so the torsion of \(X(K)\) of order prime to \(p\) injects into \(\widetilde X(\kappa)\). For an abelian variety over a number field and a finite place of good reduction, this injectivity is [HS, Theorem C.1.4].

  • (I12) Faltings [Fal, §5, Satz 3, Satz 4 and Korollar 1]. For abelian varieties \(X,Y\) over \(\Q\), \(V_\ell(X)\) is a semisimple \(G_{\Q}\)-module and \(\Hom_{\Q}(X,Y)\otimes\Z_\ell\)\({}\cong \Hom_{G_{\Q}}(T_\ell X,T_\ell Y)\). For an elliptic curve \(E_0/\Q\), \(\End_{\Q}(E_0)=\Z\) (complex multiplications are not defined over \(\Q\)), so \(V_\ell(E_0)\) is irreducible with commutant \(\Q_\ell\), and the \(\Q_\ell\)-span of the image of \(G_{\Q}\) is \(\End(V_\ell(E_0))\) (density theorem).

  • (I13) Conductors [Si2, IV §10 and Theorem IV.10.2], [BK]. For an abelian variety \(X\) over \(\Q\) and primes \(p\neq\ell\), \(v_p(N_X)\)\({}=\dim V_\ell(X)\)\({}-\dim V_\ell(X)^{I_p}+\delta_p(X)\), where the wild part \(\delta_p(X)\ge0\) vanishes if \(I_p\) acts unipotently on \(V_\ell(X)\). For an elliptic curve, \(v_p(N_E)\) is \(0\), \(1\) or at least \(2\) according as the reduction is good, multiplicative or additive.

  • (I14) Cremona's tables [Cr], [LMFDB]. We rely on the completeness of Cremona's tables of elliptic curves over \(\Q\) of conductor below \(500000\), as recorded by the LMFDB; Magma's CremonaDatabase, used in Part H of the certificate, contains these tables (largest conductor \(499999\)).

2. The gluing and the torsion of the two elliptic curves

Throughout, \((A,\lambda)\) is a principally polarized abelian surface over \(\Q\), split over \(\Q\), with \(\lambda\) defined over \(\Q\); \(E\subset A\) is an elliptic curve over \(\Q\), and \(F\), \(n\), \(\psi\), \(\sigma\), \(\alpha\), \(\pi_E\), \(\pi_F\) are as in (I10). We identify \(T_E\) and \(T_F\) with their images in \(G=A(\Q)_\tors\).

Lemma 1 (the gluing).

  1. The sequences \(0\to T_F\to G\to T_E\) and \(0\to T_E\to G\to T_F\), with the maps \(\pi_E\) and \(\pi_F\), are exact; so \(\#G\) divides \(\#T_E\cdot\#T_F\), and if \(G\) has a point of order \(p\), so does \(T_E\) or \(T_F\). For a prime \(p\), the \(p\)-rank of \(G\) is at most the sum of those of \(T_E\) and \(T_F\).

  2. If a prime \(p\) does not divide \(n\), then \(\sigma\) induces an isomorphism of Galois modules \(E[p^\infty]\times F[p^\infty]\cong A[p^\infty]\), and \(G[p^\infty]\cong T_E[p^\infty]\times T_F[p^\infty]\).

  3. If \(m\mid n\), then \(\psi\) restricts to an isomorphism of Galois modules \(E[m]\to F[m]\); in particular \(T_E[m]\cong T_F[m]\), and \(t_p(E)\equiv t_p(F)\pmod m\) at every prime \(p\nmid m\) of good reduction for \(E\) and \(F\).

  4. \(V_\ell(A)\cong V_\ell(E)\oplus V_\ell(F)\) as \(G_{\Q}\)-modules for every prime \(\ell\). If \(E\) and \(F\) have good or multiplicative reduction at \(p\), then \(v_p(N_A)=v_p(N_E)+v_p(N_F)\).

  5. (Kummer relation.) Let \(R\in A(\Q)\) and write \(R=\sigma(U,V)\) with \(U\in E(\bar\Q)\), \(V\in F(\bar\Q)\). Then \(\alpha(R)=(nU,nV)\)\({}\in E(\Q)\times F(\Q)\), and \(gV-V=\psi(gU-U)\) for every \(g\in G_{\Q}\).

Proof. (a) Take rational points in the exact sequences of abelian varieties \(0\to F\to A\to E\to0\) and \(0\to E\to A\to F\to0\) given by \(\pi_E\) and \(\pi_F\). The \(p\)-rank statement follows from \(G/T_E\hookrightarrow T_F\). (b) \(\ker\sigma\subseteq(E\times F)[n]\) has order prime to \(p\), so \(\sigma\) is injective on \((E\times F)[p^\infty]\); it is onto \(A[p^\infty]\), because a preimage of a point of \(A[p^\infty]\) is a torsion point whose component of order prime to \(p\) maps into \(A[p^\infty]\cap\ker[m]=0\) for some \(m\) prime to \(p\). Take Galois invariants. (c) \(\psi\) is a \(G_{\Q}\)-equivariant isomorphism \(E[n]\to F[n]\), and maps \(E[m]=E[n][m]\) onto \(F[m]\); a Frobenius element at \(p\) acts on \(E[m]\) and \(F[m]\) with traces \(a_p(E)\) and \(a_p(F)\) modulo \(m\). (d) \(\sigma\) is an isogeny over \(\Q\). By (I13), \(\delta_p=0\) for \(E\), \(F\) and \(A\) when \(I_p\) acts unipotently on \(V_\ell(E)\) and \(V_\ell(F)\) (\(\ell\neq p\)), which holds for good and multiplicative reduction by (I7) and (I8), and the term \(\dim V_\ell-\dim V_\ell^{I_p}\) is additive. (e) \(\alpha(R)=\alpha\sigma(U,V)=(nU,nV)\), which is rational since \(\alpha\) is defined over \(\Q\). For \(g\in G_{\Q}\), \(gR=R\) gives \(\sigma(gU-U,gV-V)=0\), so \((gU-U,gV-V)\)\({}\in\ker\sigma=\Gamma_\psi\). ∎

Proposition 3 (the torsion of the two curves). Let \(G=A(\Q)_\tors\). After exchanging \(E\) and \(F\) if necessary:

  1. if \(G\cong\Z/25\), then \(T_E\cong T_F\cong\Z/5\) and \(5\mid n\);

  2. if \(G\cong\Z/32\), then either \(T_E\cong\Z/8\), \(T_F\cong\Z/4\) and \(v_2(n)=2\), or \(T_E\cong T_F\cong\Z/8\) and \(16\mid n\);

  3. if \(G\cong\Z/2\times\Z/28\), then \(T_E\cong\Z/7\), \(T_F\cong\Z/2\times\Z/4\) and \(n\) is prime to \(14\);

  4. if \(G\cong(\Z/2)^2\times\Z/20\), then \(T_E\cong\Z/10\), \(T_F\cong\Z/2\times\Z/4\) and \(n\) is odd and prime to \(5\);

  5. if \(G\cong(\Z/2)^3\times\Z/12\), then \(T_E\cong\Z/2\times\Z/6\), \(T_F\cong\Z/2\times\Z/4\) and \(n\) is prime to \(6\).

If \(A\) is the Jacobian of a curve of genus \(2\), then \(n\ge3\) in (c) and (d), and \(n\ge5\) in (e).

Proof. By Lemma 1(a), \(T_E\) and \(T_F\) are subgroups of \(G\) from the list (I1), \(\#G\mid\#T_E\#T_F\), and \(G/T_E\) and \(G/T_F\) embed in \(T_F\) and \(T_E\). Part A of the certificate lists, for each of the five groups \(G\), all pairs of groups from (I1) with these properties; they are the pairs in (a)–(e). By hand:

  • \(G=\Z/25\): \(T_E,T_F\subseteq\Z/25\) are \(0\) or \(\Z/5\) by (I1), and \(25\mid\#T_E\#T_F\).

  • \(G=\Z/32\): \(T_E\), \(T_F\) are cyclic \(2\)-groups, so of order at most \(8\), with product of orders at least \(32\).

  • \(G=\Z/2\times\Z/28\): one of the curves, say \(E\), has a point of order \(7\), so \(T_E=\Z/7\), of \(2\)-rank \(0\); then \(T_F\) has \(2\)-rank \(2\) and embeds in \(G\), so \(T_F\in\{(\Z/2)^2,\Z/2\times\Z/4\}\), and \(56\mid7\#T_F\) gives \(T_F=\Z/2\times\Z/4\).

  • \(G=(\Z/2)^2\times\Z/20\): one curve, say \(E\), has a point of order \(5\), so \(T_E\in\{\Z/5,\Z/10\}\) has \(2\)-rank at most \(1\); as \(G\) has \(2\)-rank \(3\), \(T_E=\Z/10\) and \(T_F\) has \(2\)-rank \(2\); it embeds in \(G\), whose exponent is \(20\), and \(80\mid10\#T_F\), so \(T_F=\Z/2\times\Z/4\).

  • \(G=(\Z/2)^3\times\Z/12\): both have \(2\)-rank \(2\), and one, say \(E\), has a point of order \(3\), so \(T_E=\Z/2\times\Z/6\). Gluing degrees. If a prime \(p\) divides \(n\), then \(T_E[p]\cong T_F[p]\) by Lemma 1(c). In (c) this excludes \(p=2\) and \(p=7\); in (d) \(p=2\) and \(p=5\); in (e) \(p=3\).

Lemma 4 (stable lattices). Lemma 6 (formal groups). Let \(O\) be a complete discrete valuation ring of characteristic \(0\) with residue characteristic \(p\), normalized valuation \(v\) and \(e=v(p)\), and let \(\Phi\) be a commutative formal group law of dimension \(g\) over \(O\). For \(x\in\mathfrak m^g\) put \(v(x)=\min_iv(x_i)\).

  1. Every coefficient of \([p](X)\) is in \(pO\), except those of the monomials \(X^\gamma\) with \(\gamma\in p\mathbb N^g\smallsetminus\{0\}\). Hence \(v([p](x))\)\({}\ge\min(e+v(x),\ pv(x))\).

  2. If \(x\neq0\) and \([p](x)=0\), then \(v(x)\le e/(p-1)\).

  3. If \(e<p-1\), then \(\Phi(\mathfrak m)\) has no point of order \(p\); if \(e<p(p-1)\), it has no point of order \(p^2\).

  4. For an elliptic curve \(E/\Q_p\), \(E_1(\Q_p)\) is torsion-free if \(p\ge3\), and its torsion has order at most \(2\) if \(p=2\).

Proof. (a) Let \(\Phi_p(X_1,\dots,X_p)=\sum_\beta a_\beta X_1^{\beta_1}\cdots X_p^{\beta_p}\) be the \(p\)-fold sum; it has coefficients in \(O\) and is symmetric, by commutativity and associativity. The coefficient of \(X^\gamma\) in \([p](X)=\Phi_p(X,\dots,X)\) is \(\sum a_\beta\) over the \(\beta\) with \(\beta_1+\dots+\beta_p=\gamma\). The cyclic permutations of the \(p\) factors preserve \(a_\beta\), and their orbits have \(p\) elements except for the fixed points \(\beta_1=\dots=\beta_p\), which occur only if \(\gamma\in p\mathbb N^g\). So the coefficient lies in \(pO\) unless \(\gamma\in p\mathbb N^g\). A monomial with coefficient in \(pO\) and total degree at least \(1\) has valuation at least \(e+v(x)\) at \(x\), and one with \(\gamma\in p\mathbb N^g\smallsetminus\{0\}\) has valuation at least \(pv(x)\).

(b) Let \(s=v(x)\) and suppose \(s>e/(p-1)\); choose \(i\) with \(v(x_i)=s\). The \(i\)-th coordinate of \([p](x)\) is \(px_i\), of valuation \(e+s\), plus monomials of valuation at least \(e+2s\) (coefficient in \(pO\), degree at least \(2\)) or \(ps>e+s\). So it is nonzero.

(c) The first claim follows from (b), since \(v(x)\ge1\). If \(x\) has order \(p^2\), then \(y=[p](x)\ne0\) is killed by \(p\), and by (a), (b): \(e/(p-1)\ge v(y)\)\({}\ge\min(e+1,p)\), so \(e\ge p(p-1)\).

(d) \(E_1(\Q_p)\) is the group of points of the formal group of \(E\) over \(\Z_p\) (I6), and \(e=1\). For \(p\ge3\) use (c) and the fact that its torsion has \(p\)-power order (I6). For \(p=2\), the points with \(v\ge2\) form a torsion-free group, isomorphic to \(4\Z_2\) by the formal logarithm [Si1, Theorem IV.6.4(b)], of index \(2\) [Si1, Proposition IV.3.2(a)]. ∎

Lemma 8 (comparison of local representations). Let \(p\) be a prime, \(\ell\ge3\) a prime, and \(E\), \(F\) elliptic curves over \(\Q_p\) with good or multiplicative reduction such that \(E[\ell]\cong F[\ell]\) as \(G_{\Q_p}\)-modules.

  2. If \(\ell=p\), then \(E\) has good supersingular reduction if and only if \(F\) has. Otherwise \(u(E)=u(F)\) in \(\F_p\), where \(u(X)=a_p(X)\bmod p\) if \(X\) has good ordinary reduction and \(u(X)=\pm1\) if \(X\) has split, resp. nonsplit, multiplicative reduction.

Proof. (a) Let \(\varphi\) be a Frobenius lift. If \(X\in\{E,F\}\) has good reduction, \(X[\ell]\) is unramified and \(\varphi\) has trace \(a_p(X)\) on it; if \(X\) has multiplicative reduction, the semisimplification of \(X[\ell]\) is \(\varepsilon\oplus\varepsilon\bar\chi_\ell\) with \(\varepsilon\) trivial or \(\eta\) (I8), and \(\varphi\) has trace \(\varepsilon(\varphi)(1+p)=t_p(X)\). Isomorphic modules give equal traces. (b) By (I9), if \(X\) is supersingular then \(X[p]\) is irreducible, and otherwise the semisimplification of \(X[p]\) is \(\psi_X\oplus\bar\chi_p\psi_X^{-1}\) with \(\psi_X\) unramified, \(\bar\chi_p\psi_X^{-1}\) ramified (as \(\bar\chi_p\) has order \(p-1\ge2\) on \(I_p\)), and \(\psi_X(\varphi)=u(X)\). In the multiplicative case this is (I8). In the ordinary case, \(\psi_X\) is the action on \(\widetilde X[p](\bar\F_p)\), on which \(\varphi\) acts as the Frobenius endomorphism \(\pi\); since \(\pi\) is bijective on points and \(\hat\pi\pi=p\), the dual \(\hat\pi\) kills \(\widetilde X[p](\bar\F_p)=\pi(\widetilde X[p](\bar\F_p))\), so \(\pi=a_p-\hat\pi\) acts as \(a_p\). Lemma 18 (nonsplit multiplicative primes). In the setting of this section, there is no prime \(p\) at which \(E\) has nonsplit multiplicative reduction with \(5\nmid v_p(\Delta_{\min}(E))\) and \(v_5(p+1)=1\). The same holds for \(F\).

Proof. Suppose \(p\) is such a prime; \(p\neq5\) by Theorem 17(ii). By (I8), \(T_5(E)\cong\eta\otimes T_5(E_q)\) with \(v_p(q)=v_p(\Delta_{\min}(E))\) prime to \(5\), so some element of \(I_p\) acts on \(E[5]\) by a transvection, \(E[5]^{I_p}\) is a line, and a Frobenius lift \(\varphi\) acts on it by \(\eta(\varphi)\bar\chi_5(\varphi)=-p\)\({}\equiv1\pmod5\). As \(F[5]\cong E[5]\), \(F[5]\) is ramified at \(p\), so \(F\) is not good at \(p\) (I7), nor additive (Lemma 14(a)). If \(F\) were split, \(F[5]^{I_p}\) would be the line \(\mu_5\) (I8), on which \(\varphi\) acts by \(p\equiv-1\pmod5\); but an isomorphism \(E[5]\cong F[5]\) maps \(E[5]^{I_p}\) onto \(F[5]^{I_p}\) compatibly with \(\varphi\). So \(F\) is nonsplit, and on \(V_5(E)\) and \(V_5(F)\) the characteristic polynomial of \(\varphi\) is \(f(Y)=(Y+1)(Y+p)\) (I8).
