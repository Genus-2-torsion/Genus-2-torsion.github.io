Prime torsion on abelian surfaces split over ℚ

20 October 2026 · A consequence of Mazur's theorem

We show that the only primes dividing the order of the rational torsion subgroup of an abelian surface split over ℚ are 2, 3, 5 and 7. Here “split over ℚ” means isogenous over ℚ to a product of elliptic curves over ℚ.

Proposition

Let A/ℚ be an abelian surface split over ℚ. If a prime ℓ divides #A(ℚ)tors, then ℓ ∈ {2, 3, 5, 7}.

Proof. Choose an isogeny E₁ × E₂ → A defined over ℚ, with E₁ and E₂ elliptic curves over ℚ. The image of E₁ × {0} is an elliptic subvariety E ⊂ A defined over ℚ. The quotient F = A/E is also an elliptic curve over ℚ. Thus there is an exact sequence of abelian varieties over ℚ

0 → E → A → F → 0.

Let ℓ > 7 be prime. By Mazur's theorem, E(ℚ)[ℓ] = F(ℚ)[ℓ] = 0. For any P ∈ A(ℚ)[ℓ], its image in F(ℚ) is killed by ℓ, and hence is zero. It follows that P ∈ E(ℚ)[ℓ], so P = 0. Therefore A(ℚ)[ℓ] = 0. By Cauchy's theorem, ℓ cannot divide #A(ℚ)tors. ∎

Consequences for the census

Reference

B. Mazur, Modular curves and the Eisenstein ideal, Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 47 (1977), 33–186. Mazur's classification gives E(ℚ)tors ≅ ℤ/nℤ for 1 ≤ n ≤ 10 or n = 12, or ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/2mℤ for 1 ≤ m ≤ 4. In particular, the only possible prime orders of rational torsion points are 2, 3, 5 and 7.
