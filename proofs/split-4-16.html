Infinitely many split genus-2 Jacobians with rational torsion \(\Z/4\Z\times\Z/16\Z\)

20 October 2026 · ∞ exact torsion

We give an explicit rational family of smooth genus-2 curves over \(\Q\) whose Jacobians split over \(\Q\) and have rational torsion subgroup exactly \(\Z/4\Z\times\Z/16\Z\). The family contains infinitely many geometric isomorphism classes. Exactness follows from the intersection of the torsion Kummer images of the two elliptic factors.

1. The family

For \(s\in\Q\), put

\[ t=t(s)=-\frac{3(3s^2-10s+5)^2+(3s^2-5)^2}{4(3s^2-5)^2}, \qquad D(t)=256t^2+47t+16. \]

Let \(C_s\) be the smooth projective model of

\[ y^2=D(t)(16x^2+9t) \bigl(175x^2-9(4t-1)^2\bigr) \bigl(9x^2+(4t+1)^2\bigr). \]

Theorem. For every \(s\in\Q\), the curve \(C_s\) has genus \(2\), its Jacobian is isogenous over \(\Q\) to a product of elliptic curves, and

\[ \Jac(C_s)(\Q)_{\tors}\simeq\Z/4\Z\times\Z/16\Z. \]

The curves \(C_s\), with \(s\in\Q\), contain infinitely many \(\overline{\Q}\)-isomorphism classes.

We prove exactness for every rational parameter. In particular, the theorem requires no exceptional thin set or congruence restriction.

2. Parameters and elliptic gluing

Put

\[ a=\frac{3(3s^2-10s+5)}{2(3s^2-5)},\qquad b=-\frac{5(3s^2-6s+5)}{2(3s^2-5)}. \]

A direct calculation gives

\[ a^2=-3(t+1/4),\qquad b^2=-5(t-1/4),\qquad 6b^2-10a^2=15. \]

The polynomials \(3s^2-5\), \(3s^2-10s+5\), and \(3s^2-6s+5\) have no rational zeros. Hence \(a,b\ne0\) and \(t<-1/4\) for every rational \(s\). The discriminant of \(D\) is \(-14175\), so \(D(t)>0\) for real \(t\).

Let \(m=t^2-1/16\), and consider

\[ \begin{aligned} E_t &: y^2+xy-my=x^3-mx^2,\\ F &: y^2+\frac{47}{42}xy+\frac{5}{49}y=x^3+\frac{5}{49}x^2. \end{aligned} \]

After completing the square in \(y\), their monic cubic models have the following ordered roots:

\[ \begin{aligned} (e_1,e_2,e_3)&=(m,-t/2-1/8,t/2-1/8),\\ (f_1,f_2,f_3)&=(-15/112,-6/49,-10/63). \end{aligned} \]

The roots are distinct for \(t<-1/4\). Let \(\alpha:E_t[2]\longrightarrow F[2]\) send the point at \(e_i\) to the point at \(f_i\), and let \(\Gamma\) be its graph.

We apply the gluing construction of Howe–Leprévost–Poonen, Propositions 3 and 4. In their notation,

\[ a_2=\sum_{i=1}^3 e_i(f_{i+2}-f_{i+1})=-\frac{D(t)}{7056}\ne0, \]

where subscripts are taken modulo \(3\). Thus the correspondence of roots is not induced by an affine change of the elliptic \(x\)-coordinate, and \(\alpha\) is not the restriction of an isomorphism of elliptic curves over \(\overline{\Q}\). Consequently

\[ J=(E_t\times F)/\Gamma \]

with its induced principal polarization is a genus-2 Jacobian. Write

\[ \phi:E_t\times F\longrightarrow J,\qquad \psi:J\longrightarrow E_t\times F \]

for the gluing isogeny and its dual, so that \(\psi\phi=[2]\) and \(\phi\psi=[2]\).

The quotient is precisely the Jacobian of the displayed family. For completeness, let \(h(X)\) be the sextic in Howe–Leprévost–Poonen, Proposition 4, using the root order above. Its constants \(A,B\) are

\[ A=-\frac{25D(t)}{17076987648},\qquad B=\frac{t(4t-1)^2(4t+1)^2D(t)}{1806336}. \]

If \(g_t(x)\) denotes the right-hand side of the family equation, then

\[ h(7x)=\left(\frac{Atm^2}{12096}\right)^2g_t(x). \]

All factors in this change of variables are nonzero for our parameters. The sextic discriminant is

\[ \operatorname{disc}_x(g_t)= 56171432824012800\,t(4t-1)^2(4t+1)^2D(t)^{22}\ne0. \]

Thus every \(C_s\) is smooth of genus \(2\), and its Jacobian splits over \(\Q\).

3. The torsion Kummer images

We use multiplicative notation for \(\Q^\times/\Q^{\times2}\). On an elliptic curve with monic cubic model \(Y^2=\prod_{i=1}^3(x-r_i)\), the \(2\)-Kummer map is

\[ \delta:E(\Q)/2E(\Q)\longrightarrow(\Q^\times/\Q^{\times2})^3. \]

For a point away from the nonzero \(2\)-torsion, it is represented by \((x-r_1,x-r_2,x-r_3)\). At the \(2\)-torsion point with \(x=r_i\), the \(i\)-th component is \(\prod_{j\ne i}(r_i-r_j)\), and the other components are \(r_i-r_j\). The identity has image \((1,1,1)\). We use the ordered roots above for the coordinate order on the two curves, so that their Kummer coordinates agree under \(\alpha\).

Let \(P=(0,0)\) on the displayed generalized Weierstrass model of \(E_t\), and let \(Q=(0,0)\) on that of \(F\). Let \(T\in E_t[2]\) have \(x=e_2\), and let \(U\in F[2]\) have \(x=f_3\). The addition law gives

\[ \operatorname{ord}(P)=4,\quad x(2P)=e_1,\qquad \operatorname{ord}(Q)=8,\quad x(4Q)=f_2. \]

In particular, \(P,T\) generate a subgroup of type \([2,4]\), and \(Q,U\) generate a subgroup of type \([2,8]\).

The Kummer images, as triples of square classes, are

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta_E(P)&=(-m,\,2(t+1/4),\,-2(t-1/4)),& \delta_E(T)&=(-1,t,-t),\\ \delta_F(Q)&=(105,6,70),& \delta_F(U)&=(-7,-1,7). \end{aligned} \]

By the conic identities, \(m=a^2b^2/15\). Therefore

\[ \delta_E(P)=(-15,-6,10)=\delta_F(Q+U). \]

Lemma. For every rational \(s\),

\[ \begin{aligned} E_t(\Q)_{\tors}&=\langle P,T\rangle\simeq\Z/4\Z\times\Z/2\Z,\\ F(\Q)_{\tors}&=\langle Q,U\rangle\simeq\Z/8\Z\times\Z/2\Z. \end{aligned} \]

Proof. Mazur's theorem gives the assertion for \(F\). For \(E_t\), the only possibilities are \([2,4]\) and \([2,8]\). In the latter case its four points of order \(4\) are \(\pm P,\pm(P+T)\), and some such point is twice a rational point of order \(8\). However, the first Kummer coordinates of \(P\) and \(P+T\) have square classes \(-15\) and \(15\), respectively. Neither is a square in \(\Q\). Their negatives have the same Kummer images, so none of these points is divisible by \(2\) in \(E_t(\Q)\). This excludes \([2,8]\). □

Put \(T_E=E_t(\Q)_{\tors}\) and \(T_F=F(\Q)_{\tors}\). The first coordinates of their Kummer images are, respectively,

\[ \{1,-15,-1,15\},\qquad \{1,105,-7,-15\}. \]

The two sets have exactly the common square classes \(1\) and \(-15\). By the matching identity, the complete triples with first coordinate \(-15\) also agree. It follows that

\[ \delta_E(T_E)\cap\delta_F(T_F)=\{(1,1,1),(-15,-6,10)\}. \]

4. Exact torsion of the Jacobian

Define

\[ M=\{(A,B)\in T_E\times T_F:\delta_E(A)=\delta_F(B)\}. \]

We claim that there is an exact sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow\ker(\psi)(\Q) \longrightarrow J(\Q)_{\tors} \xrightarrow{\ \psi\ }M\longrightarrow0. \]

Indeed, choose halves \(\widetilde A,\widetilde B\) of \(A,B\). The point \(\phi(\widetilde A,\widetilde B)\) is rational if and only if every Galois difference of the pair \((\widetilde A,\widetilde B)\) lies in \(\Gamma\). Since \(E_t[2]\) and \(F[2]\) are rational, this is exactly the equality of Kummer classes in the definition of \(M\). It is then a lift of \((A,B)\) under \(\psi\). The lift is torsion: either this follows from its construction using halves of torsion points, or from \(2R=\phi\psi(R)\). Conversely, every rational lift gives the equality of Kummer classes. This proves the exact sequence.

The group scheme

\[ \ker(\psi)=\phi(E_t[2]\times F[2]) \simeq(E_t[2]\times F[2])/\Gamma \]

is constant of order \(4\). The kernel of the Kummer map on the torsion subgroup of an elliptic curve is twice that torsion subgroup, since a half of a torsion point is torsion. Hence the lemma and the Kummer intersection give

\[ \#M=\#(2T_E)\#(2T_F) \#\bigl(\delta_E(T_E)\cap\delta_F(T_F)\bigr) =2\cdot4\cdot2=16. \]

By the exact sequence,

\[ \#J(\Q)_{\tors}=4\cdot16=64. \]

It remains to determine the group structure. Put \(V=Q+U\), which has order \(8\), and

\[ X=\phi(P,0),\qquad Y=\phi(0,V). \]

The graph \(\Gamma\) is generated by \((2P,U+4V)\) and \((T,4V)\). In the quotient of \(T_E\times T_F\), these relations eliminate the generators corresponding to \(T\) and \(U\). The remaining relations are \(4X=0\) and \(8Y=0\). Thus

\[ H:=\phi(T_E\times T_F)=\langle X,Y\rangle \simeq\Z/4\Z\times\Z/8\Z. \]

The Kummer match gives \(R\in J(\Q)_{\tors}\) with \(\psi(R)=(P,V)\). It satisfies

\[2R=X+Y.\]

The point \(X+Y\) has order \(8\), so \(R\) has order \(16\). Moreover, \(R\notin H\), since \(2H\) has exponent \(4\). Therefore \(\langle H,R\rangle\) has order \(64\), and by the order calculation it is the whole rational torsion subgroup. It is generated by \(X,R\), since \(Y=2R-X\). The resulting surjection

\[ \Z/4\Z\times\Z/16\Z\longrightarrow J(\Q)_{\tors} \]

is an isomorphism because both groups have order \(64\).

5. Variation in moduli

The varying elliptic factor has

\[ j(E_t)=\frac{(256t^4+224t^2+1)^3}{t^2(4t-1)^4(4t+1)^4}. \]

This rational function is nonconstant, and so is \(t(s)\). Thus \(j(E_{t(s)})\) assumes infinitely many values for \(s\in\Q\). The gluing construction supplies a degree-\(2\) map \(C_s\longrightarrow E_t\) over \(\Q\). A fixed smooth genus-2 curve over \(\overline{\Q}\) has finitely many automorphisms, and hence finitely many involutions and degree-\(2\) elliptic quotients up to isomorphism. Consequently the infinitely many values of \(j(E_{t(s)})\) force infinitely many geometric isomorphism classes of \(C_s\). This proves the theorem. □

6. Reproducibility and the census example

The accompanying standalone Magma certificate verifies the elliptic point orders, all Kummer identities, the HLP change of variables, the discriminant, the conic parametrization, and the elliptic \(j\)-formula. All assertions passed in Magma V2.30-1; the verification log is available. The proof above uses only exact identities, elliptic torsion classification, and the stated gluing theorem.

At \(s=1\), we have \(t=-1\), and after dividing \(y\) by \(225\) the curve becomes

\[ y^2=(16x^2-9)(7x^2-9)(x^2+1). \]

The change of variables \(z=3/x\), \(w=3y/x^3\) gives

\[w^2=z^6-14z^4-95z^2+1008.\]

Writing \(w=2v+z^3+z\) gives the census example 4.16.a,

\[v^2+(z^3+z)v=-4z^4-24z^2+252.\]

Magma independently computes exact torsion \([4,16]\) for this specialization and for \(s=2\), where \(t=-19/49\). These specialization checks are consistency checks; they are not needed for the uniform torsion argument.
